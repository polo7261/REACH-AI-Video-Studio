# gatsby-artdeco-style

Use this skill when generating videos/images with old-Hollywood golden tones, Art Deco, and Americana/Route 66 highway vibes, or when applying this aesthetic to any subject matter such as history/food/sports/romance/suspense/MV/dreams/road trips. **Style homage only; no claim to be any specific copyrighted work/artist.**

> Environment mappings: GPT Image/Seedance/MultiModalToVideo → `generate_image_v3`/`generate_video_v3`; text to narration/BGM → `generate_audio`; text_editor → `write_file_v2`; video_assembler → `composite_video`. Prompt parameters (2K/720p/aspect ratio/film vocabulary) are craft-level wording — retain all. Super-resolution post-processing (Jimeng SR 4K/MediaKit 1080p) = no corresponding capability in this environment, remove.

## Workflow (pause at milestones, do not run through in one pass)

1. **Parameters**: `write_file_v2` locks subject type/duration/language/aspect ratio (**2.39:1 preferred** or 16:9)/visual style ("golden nostalgia").
2. **Storyboard**〔pause〕: key elements / shot list / audio layers; after completion, scan the context and bind user-uploaded or previously generated assets to the corresponding slots.
3. **Visual elements**〔pause〕: generate images for all key elements (bind directly if reference images already exist); **for characters with dialogue, process key_element_audio immediately after the element images are completed** (bind directly if the user provides a voice reference, otherwise generate a short sample based on the voice style) — must be completed before generating video for any shot containing dialogue.
4. **Per-shot video**〔pause〕: use the element image as the primary reference; only append the previous shot's reference_video when visual continuity with the previous shot is extremely strong.
5. **Audio** (BGM + VO narration)〔pause〕→ 6. `composite_video` to composite and export.

Dependencies: 2→1; 3→2; 4→3; 5→2; 6→3,4,5. Pause and wait for confirmation after each main stage (Spec lock/storyboard/element images/shot videos/audio). **Bind user-uploaded assets to the corresponding slots first to avoid duplicate generation.**

## Reference Asset Analysis (6 items)

When assets are uploaded, analyze item by item: ① **Color alignment**: extract dominant color tones, determine whether they are close to the golden nostalgia palette, and output the deviation from the target palette for prompt correction. ② **Lighting style identification**: whether Golden Hour backlight / volumetric Tyndall light / Anamorphic horizontal flares / shallow depth of field are present; if missing, add to a supplemental checklist. ③ **Camera language identification**: aspect ratio/shot size/camera movement type as the storyboard baseline. ④ **Character/scene element extraction**: costume, hairstyle, and makeup; period feel of architecture and furnishings; signature props → structured descriptions used directly for key_element. ⑤ **Period accuracy check**: exclude modern elements that break the 1920s–1950s setting (phones/LED/modern architecture/contemporary car models); if present, exclude them or push them into a blurred background. ⑥ **Americana highway element identification**: road surface type / proportion of horizon vanishing point / roadside signs, gas stations, billboards, Diners / era of car chrome details / light incidence angle → used for highway-type element scenes.

## Aesthetic Tone (Global Lock)

- **Fusion palette**: champagne gold + tobacco brown + emerald green + ivory white + sunset crimson. (Teal&Orange / Arri Alexa grading is not the global default; selected per shot only for specific subjects, never written into the global setting.)
- **Aspect ratio**: prefer **2.39:1 Anamorphic widescreen**, with 16:9 as the second choice.
- **Lighting**: Golden Hour backlight / volumetric light (visible dust) / Anamorphic horizontal elliptical flares / indoor candlelight or amber tungsten point light.
- **Film texture scene grading**: mansions/banquets/stages use fine **35mm** grain (stable exposure, deep blacks while preserving costume texture); memories/highways/motels/private footage switch to **16mm** grain (slight exposure drift, occasional frame jumps, vertical scratches). **Never mix the two grain types within the same scene type.**
- **Core emotions (must be expressed through visible results, not abstract atmosphere words)**: empty wine glasses + scattered sequins after the banquet; a character standing alone at the edge of a wide room; lovers in the same frame but with gazes that never meet; gazing at each other through car windows/door frames/water surfaces/crowds; distant lights/figures that can never truly be approached.

## Dual Visual Grammar (Global Lock)

The two visual languages must alternate within the same video; a single state throughout is not allowed:
- **Public occasions** (banquets/mansions/stage/high society spaces): stable or slow moving camera; Art Deco symmetrical composition; black and ivory white as the base, with champagne gold only as local highlight accents (never covering everything); restrained expressions, contained movements; fine 35mm grain, no handheld shake.
- **Private moments** (memories/highways/motels/solitude): slight handheld or off-center composition; faded warm white and tobacco brown as dominant tones; natural exposure drift allowed; heavier 16mm grain, with occasional frame jumps and vertical scratches retained to preserve the private-footage feel.
- **Mandatory contrast rhythm**: after every banquet/mansion/jewelry/luxury car scene, a shot must immediately follow of a character alone / an empty seat / a leftover drink glass / an extinguished lamp / an empty highway — using visual contrast to carry the theme of disillusionment, never letting the glamorous state run through the entire piece.

## Key Elements Design Specifications

- **Character elements**: costume/makeup/prop details (hairstyle/makeup/fabric materials) + emotional tone; list multiple looks/age groups one by one (Look A/Look B).
- **Voice binding (key_element_audio)**: any character with dialogue in a shot must have key_element_audio defined; bind a voice reference audio clip to maintain timbre across shots, and note the voice style in the description (presets in references/audio-voice-presets.md may be referenced). **Samples must be 8–12 seconds and cover the character's typical emotional range** (calm narration + emotionally fluctuating lines + a subdued ending), not single-emotion reading — single-emotion samples will cause unstable TTS timbre in shots with different emotions. If a user-uploaded reference has a single emotion, note it so multi-emotion coverage can be supplemented.
- **Scene elements**: architectural style (Art Deco mansion/old Hollywood studio/jazz nightclub/Long Island bay dock) or Americana highway imagery (the far end of a Route 66 road/desert highway backlight/1950s gas station/faded billboard/roadside Diner/motel neon night scene); note the main light source position and color temperature + signature Americana details (asphalt heat-haze distortion, roadside weeds, horizon vanishing point, rusted signs with faded neon, rotating bar jukebox); highway scenes must note the shooting perspective — in-car subjective (windshield framing) or exterior low-angle tracking shot (road surface + tires).
- **Prop elements** (key props listed as separate items for easy close-up reference): indoor/party type — champagne tower/vintage gramophone/faded old photos and love letters/crystal candelabra/period white telephone; highway Americana type — 1950s front-end chrome trim/steering wheel, glove box, vintage dashboard/rusted Route 66 sign/faded billboard/Diner neon sign/road map and leather suitcase/gas station weathervane.
- **User-uploaded assets**: element descriptions must match the visible content in the images, with no contradictions.

## Shot Design Specifications

- **Duration rhythm**: normal narrative shots 4–8 seconds; extend to 8–15 seconds only when actions such as a complete dance/continuous walking/multi-round dialogue cannot be interrupted — do not default to long shots just because the model supports 15 seconds. **One shot carries only one main action or relationship change**; shots containing multiple shot sizes/multiple subjects/multiple action outcomes must be split.
- **Four essential elements per shot**: ① Scene (referencing the element scene ID); ② Story beat (character actions/acting details/dialogue lines; dialogue is marked with {台词} to preserve the original text and accompanied by emotional beat annotations, see below); ③ Camera language (shot size + camera angle low-angle/eye-level/Dutch angle + camera movement locked/slow push-in/jib rise/Dolly zoom/Rack focus); ④ Light and shadow beat (light source type + color tone phase: high-saturation golden backlight/candlelight warmth/frame gradually yellowing and fading).

## Dialogue Emotional Beat Annotation Standard

Whenever {台词} appears in a shot, an emotional annotation must immediately follow the text; otherwise downstream models will read it flat and lose nuance. Annotations are written outside the dialogue brackets:
- **Emotional state**: (voice tightening, forcing calm) (whisper, breathiness fading) (tone suddenly hardening, volume rising), etc.
- **Rhythm and pauses**: Within dialogue, use 「——」 to mark a medium pause and 「… …」 to mark a longer emotional gap; line breaks between sentences represent natural phrasing.
- **Stress and breathiness**: (stress this word) (whole line delivered breathily, almost whispering) (trailing syllable deliberately drawn out, with reluctance).
- **Whole-line emotional arc**: Mark the trajectory before the dialogue, e.g., 【from casual nonchalance → suddenly choking up mid-sentence】.
- **Binding to micro-expression beats**: Dialogue annotations must correspond to the micro-expression descriptions of the same story beat (e.g., "corners of the mouth slightly raised but eyes desolate (understated, trailing tone sinking) {……}").
- **Difference from narration annotations**: Narration emotional annotations apply to prompts for independent audio_layer generation; dialogue emotional annotations apply to acting beats within a shot, jointly driving character performance generation via key_element_audio voice reference + emotional directives. The two systems run in parallel and do not replace each other.

## Slow Motion and VFX Restraint

- **Slow motion beats**: Only mark "120fps slow motion" for emotional climaxes or narratively significant actions (sweat/steam/a look back); ordinary blocking/dialogue/transitions are not marked. Lingering empty shots are marked "12fps slight frame-skip feel", not exceeding 20% of the shot duration.
- **VFX restraint**: All visual effects serve the narrative, not decoration. Anamorphic flares appear naturally only when strong backlight enters the frame directly, and are never deliberately designed as independent "streaking across the frame" events; volumetric light must have a realistic light source basis (window gap/door gap/stage light); film scratches and skipped frames serve as accents and do not cover main narrative passages.

## Genre Storyboard Rhythm Preferences (10 Templates)

**Candidate shot library, not a fixed order**: Select 3–5 shot types from the corresponding genre library; do not add plot-irrelevant prop close-ups/freeze frames/flares/looks back just to fit the template; every shot must fulfill one clear function (establishing space/advancing action/changing relationships/revealing information/emotional reaction); beautiful but functionless shots are not kept. The complete 10 templates (historical/food/sports/romance/mystery/MV/fantasy dream with dedicated sound design/mystery × road movie with color tone coverage + 5-part rhythm + 4 prohibited items/road movie) are in **references/genre-templates.md**.

## OP/ED Audio and Cross-Shot Audio Tracks

- **BGM**: At least one global track; long videos (3 minutes or longer) are split into multiple segments by section emotion, each with an audio_id and time range.
- **OP tone**: Energetic entrance, high rhythmic density, matching fast montage (0.5–1 second/shot). **ED tone**: Melancholic afterglow, relaxed tempo, paired with fading and heavier grain.
- **VO narration**: If there is narration, narration_speaker_profile must be written into the audio_layer description and layout_instruction must be bound to shot ranges. **Default voice, choose one of two**: Lyrical melancholic tone (default, languid soft female voice/high breathiness/dreamlike murmur) suits MV stream-of-consciousness poetic monologue; narrative documentary tone (second choice, warm weathered male voice/steady/faintly melancholic radio smoothness) suits story-driven multi-segment retrospective narration. Dream drama special: prefer the dreamy ethereal female voice; male/guide characters use the dream whisper male voice; dream segments and reality segments are bound to separate segments within the same audio_layer using different profiles.
- **Narration emotional annotations (required)**: Every narration segment must be accompanied by emotional delivery directives (emotional state/rhythm pauses/stress and breathiness/whole-segment arc); otherwise TTS will read it flat. Dream segments are additionally marked with 「suspended feeling」「dissolving feeling」; strong closing pauses are forbidden, and the voice must evaporate naturally in the air without landing. (See references/audio-voice-presets.md for 11 voice presets + narration emotion examples.)
- **3 OP/ED storyboard structure templates** (ED 15s / ED 30s / OP 30s second-level timelines) in references/genre-templates.md.

## Media Generation Specifications

- **Character reference images**: One **horizontal side-by-side image** per character — left side head-and-shoulders close-up (locks face/skin tone/hairline) + right side full body (outfit/hairstyle/makeup/footwear silhouette). For multiple outfits, use ImageToImage with the first version as reference to preserve identity.
- **3 types of shot video reference inputs**: ① Element images (required, all character elements in frame + relevant scene/prop elements); ② key_element_audio (required when there is dialogue, referencing the same voice reference for the same character across shots); ③ Previous shot's reference_video (optional, only when visual continuity is extremely strong). **Continuity judgment**: Only same character + same location + no time jump + action directly linking counts as "extremely strong continuity"; scene changes/time jumps/emotional turns do not need it.
- **Realistic restrained de-escalation**: Prioritize film-textured natural realism, not visual spectacle; if the Storyboard contains exaggerated VFX language ("flares filling the frame" "particle burst"), tone it down to a restrained version before passing into the prompt (keep the effect present but reduce its intensity).
- **Audio generation**: BGM prompts avoid the real names of well-known artists; **VO narration prompt = two parts**: ① complete narration_speaker_profile voice description + ② emotional delivery directives extracted from the audio_layer emotional annotations; missing either, the model falls back to a flat reading delivery. **4 rules for key_element_audio samples**: 8–12 seconds covering a typical emotional range; the sample is likewise written with the complete profile + emotional annotation; the same character references the same resource_id throughout the film (if unsatisfactory, regenerate and update the binding, never mixing different versions); before binding, it is recommended to play the sample to verify the voice matches the preset; regenerate if the deviation is obvious.

## Prompt Writing (Images/Video)

- **Language**: When interacting in Chinese, prompt body text is written in Chinese; dialogue/narration is kept in the original language per the Spec's output language.
- **Aesthetic keyword core library + 5 rules for single-shot selection** (core gate for this skill's prompt quality): five candidate keyword libraries — color/lighting/optics/film grading/style tags; **write in only the 1 most relevant item per category, no stacking or piling up**; do not write Anamorphic flares without a backlight source; do not write volumetric light shafts / floating dust motes without smoke, dust, or haze media; ordinary character close-ups do not default to dust motes/halo/golden rim light; if the scene's lighting is plain, faithfully describe the actual light source instead of forcing golden backlight. The complete keyword library is in **references/prompt-keyword-library.md**.
- **Color-grading mood control, 5 items** (the mood comes from precise control of shadow density and color separation; none of the items may be omitted): crushed blacks + lifted shadow floor (raise the shadow floor to preserve detail instead of clipping to pure black, with shadows shifting toward cool green/deep smoky brown); warm highlight roll-off (highlights transition gently to champagne gold/amber without blowing out); warm-cool color separation (warm highlights + cool shadows as complements, midtone saturation moderately reduced); midground atmospheric haze (not a foreground smoke effect; light dust must look like practically shot dust, not digital particles); reference color-look anchors Kodak Vision3 500T (night/interior) or 250D (daylight/Golden Hour).
- **Effects restraint, 6 items + banned words**: anamorphic flares must not be written as "bursting/sweeping across/filling the frame"; slow motion only at the climax; film scratches and skipped frames "occasional", not covering >20% of the duration; volumetric light dust must be backed by a realistic light source; avoid digital-feeling transitions such as abrupt saturation surges/drops, instant overexposure, and color inversion; **banned words**: neon glow / electric color / oversaturated / HDR tone-mapped / digital color grade / VFX particle burst.
- **Image prompt structure**: write in order as a natural-language paragraph, without outputting segmented headings — 【subject + action + scene environment】→【lighting and color tone, with matching phrases taken from the core library】→【camera language: shot size + camera movement + composition technique】→【aspect ratio & quality: 2.39:1, cinematic, 4K】→【style tags, taken from the core library】. Write only what is visible, not inner psychology; characters photorealistic and cinematic (not anime-style); for sheer clothing, use non-suggestive wording such as "gauzy mist-veiled gown"; for in-frame text, write the full renderable string inside quotation marks.
- **Video prompt structure**: reference-image placeholders such as <<<image_1>>> are placed at the beginning/at the subject to mark character identity; motion stack order: camera movement → subject action and performance beats → spatial/environmental changes → audio annotations; **Seedance wrapping syntax**: music(...), sound effects<...>, dialogue{verbatim dialogue line} (when not in the project's language, note the language before the braces), title/subtitles【...】; inside {} write only the dialogue, no descriptions; **fixed negative prompts**: if there is a separate VO track, append no narration voice over at the end; always append no music and no subtitles; for slow motion write "slow motion 120fps"/"slow motion", not wall-clock time segment cuts.
- **Character-styling reference-image section** (8 categories of period-styling lexicon — Gatsby Girl/Daisy/Old Hollywood starlet/jazz singer/road-drifter man & woman/tycoon man/power-elite man — plus general period-feel overlay words, including dual-view structure requirements): see **references/prompt-keyword-library.md**.
- **Genre-specific keyword supplements, 10 sets** (including the full English lexicon for suspense × road narrative): see **references/prompt-keyword-library.md**.

## Assembly (video_assembler)

- **Aspect ratio**: the timeline prefers 2.39:1; when platform constraints require 16:9, add top and bottom black bars (letterbox) to preserve the cinematic feel.
- **Pacing curve**: the ED is gentle overall, empty/scenery shots make up 60%+ of the ED's total duration, with brightness and saturation fading gradually (gold → yellowed → approaching black-and-white), and a closing black-screen subtitle segment ≥5 seconds; the OP's first 5 seconds are a slow-paced build-up (black-and-white/heavy grain), then cut to a fast montage (0.5–1 second per shot, beats aligned to BGM drum hits), ending with a freeze-frame + title card; for the main feature, transitions prefer dissolve or hard cut, slow push-in dissolves may be used for emotional turns, and flashy transitions are avoided.
- **Audio mixing**: BGM fades out continuously through the ED segment, dropping to 0 in the final 3 seconds, and fades in from silence during the OP; **VO narration takes priority over BGM — BGM automatically ducks to -12dB during spoken narration**; in-shot generated sound effects/dialogue are staggered against VO narration to avoid overlap.
- **Film grain layer**: for 720p assets, an overall quality upscale after compositing is recommended (this environment has no MediaKit super-resolution; state this honestly); overlay a single uniform light film grain layer on the final timeline to keep the whole film's texture consistent.
