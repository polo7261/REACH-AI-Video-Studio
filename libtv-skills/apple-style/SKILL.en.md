## II. Shot Techniques (Model Shots as the Base + Detail Shots Interwoven)

### A. Model Shots (With-Model Mode · Base)

> **★ Focus Principle (hard constraint)**: When the model interacts with the product, **the focus and visual center always stay on the product** — the product is sharp, sits at the visual center of gravity, and is emphasized by light and framing; **the model's face and appearance serve only as support/complement** (handling atmosphere, temperament, proportions, and realism), never stealing the show. Composition and focus prioritize the product: shallow depth of field blurs the model while solid focus rests on the product; the person may appear only partially in frame (hand, shoulder, side of the face, the area where the product is worn).

> **★★ Interaction Judgment (hard constraint · figure out "how a person uses it" before writing the shots)**: **It is forbidden to have the person stand stiffly merely "holding/raising" the product with no movement and no expression.** Before writing a model shot, you must first determine the product's **real use, common scenarios, correct grip/wear/contact position, which hand/body part is needed, and how the person's gaze and body weight should naturally follow**, then devise a **specific, motivated action and situation** that gets the person genuinely "using it," with **clear expression/emotion**. For example:
>
> - Headphones → putting them on, nodding to the beat / eyes closed, immersed / dancing along to the rhythm
> - Car/keys → pulling the door open and getting in, starting the ignition, gripping the wheel and pulling away / speeding
> - Hair dryer → picking it up and blow-drying the hair, hair swept by the airflow
> - Camera → raising the camera to frame a shot, pressing the shutter to take a photo, then reviewing it with a satisfied look
> - Lipstick/makeup → applying in the mirror, pressing the lips, examining the effect
> - Sneakers → tying the laces, sprinting / leaping airborne
> - Phone → unlocking, swiping, raising to shoot, reacting to the content
> ****Action Plausibility Check (must be done first)**: `Who uses it, in what scenario, and why → correct grip/wear/contact points → wind-up → contact/operation → feedback/result → gaze, shoulders, elbows, wrists, body weight and micro-expressions following naturally → the product always in focus`. The action must include preparation, operation, a pause for feedback, and natural expression; no statue-like standing holding the object, no fingers hovering in mid-air, no stiff body, no incorrect usage. If full-body use is implausible, automatically switch to a hand/half-body/partial-wear shot or a no-model hero + details; never force a model in.
> ****Must Show the Complete Usage Process (hard constraint)**: In with-model mode, the video must capture the person's **complete process of using the product** (start → mid-use → result/reaction); it must not give only a "holding/static display" frame, nor skip the usage action and cut straight to details. The usage process itself is the content's main storyline.

- **Actions must be specific and coherent**: Spell out the micro-action chain of start → process → result (e.g., "pick up the camera → raise it to the eye to frame → press the shutter → lower the camera and smile reviewing the playback"), while also describing the grip/contact points, the gaze, and how the shoulders, elbows, wrists, or body weight follow along — not a static pose.
- **Expressions must be on point**: Immersed, focused, amazed, satisfied, enjoying it... matched to the action, natural and believable; **no blank, deadpan faces and no exaggerated weirdness**.
- **The product remains the focus**: The action serves "getting the product used and seen"; focus pressed onto the product with shallow depth of field blurring the person, who may appear only partially in frame.
- Use `{{Mixed N}}` to bind the selected model, consistent throughout.

### B. Product Detail Shots (Interwoven · Material-Led)

1. **Macro Material Close-up (main workhorse)**: Extreme macro with one detail per shot; grazing light sweeps across the surface to **reveal the true material** — brushed metal, anodization, glass Fresnel reflections, ceramic glaze, woven texture. The main source of a product film's premium feel.
2. **Macro Fly-through (Macro Fly-through)**: The camera flies rapidly over the mesh / material surface.
3. **Reveal from Black (Reveal from Black)**: A hero entrance in which the product is gradually lit out of pure black and slowly emerges (no starfield/deep space).
4. **Slow Half Orbit (Half Orbit)**: Orbit the product slowly and evenly for **at most half a circle** (≤180°), admiring its form and contours.

> **No 3D teardown**: No 3D exploded views, no internal cutaways/see-through透视, no shots shuttling into the product's interior — none of these "teardown/look-inside" shots. The product is presented through **external materials, light and shadow, and usage actions**.
>
> Typical sequence: **Model using it (focus on the product) → half orbit → macro material details → macro fly-through → end card**.
>
> How to write the detail section: **One-way push-in toward the product (push only, never pull back) → showcase each part's details one by one (one per shot, material macro, optionally with at most a half-circle micro-orbit) → macro fly-through**, with continuous shots and no frame jumps; push and pull belong to separate shots; **no entering the interior, no disassembly**.

### C. Camera Movement Essentials (Distilled from High-Quality Prompts)

- **Dark-field macro light sweep**: Ultra-high-detail macro on a pure-black/dark gradient background; a sharp silver rim light or grazing light slowly sweeps across the product's edges, holes, and textures, casting deep geometric shadows within the structure and capturing the tactile texture of foam/fabric/metal/ceramic; high contrast, slow motion, and no large-area illumination.
- **Linear highlight sweeping the material**: A close-up locked on a metal/glass/ceramic section, letting a linear highlight glide slowly along the surface to reveal brushed texture, chrome plating, Fresnel reflections, and realistic highlight falloff.
- **Full product unveiling**: The product rises slowly from below or from darkness, turns from a three-quarter side view to the front and locks in centered; keep the same dark-toned light sweep — edge-defining only, never washing out to white — floating elegantly, with no lunging at the lens and no mechanical jitter.
- **Multi-point linked close-ups**: Full-view opening → linked half orbit → 3 focal close-ups (keeping a distance of about 15–20cm, no in-your-face shoving) → pull back to the full view. Transitions use the product's rotation, camera turns, panning, and rising to naturally "slide into" the next focal point; once a focal point is locked, the product briefly holds steady.
- **Division of motion**: While the camera moves, the product may briefly hold steady; while the product rotates, the camera glides along with it; finally, a fast but smooth pull-back returns to the wide-angle full view, the product frozen in its best display pose, with a subtle brightness tweak for a brand-feel finish.

## III. Background & Light (Dark Solid/Gradient Presets)

**Background (uniform across the whole film; gradients never based on white)**

- **Pure black**: A 100% pure-black seamless background, with the product and model sculpted out of the darkness by light.
- **Black-orange gradient**: black → deep burnt orange / blazing orange, like embers in a dark scene; used only as background color temperature, never stealing focus from the product.
- **Black-red gradient**: black → deep red / wine red, strong dramatic tension; suits headphones, perfume, cars, and trendy tech.
- **Black-gold gradient**: black → dark gold / amber gold, luxury-leaning; suits beauty, perfume, and watches.
- **Black-gray smoke**: black → charcoal gray, the lowest-risk option, preserving a minimalist premium feel.

> No starry sky/deep space, vast space, studio scenes, or geometric decorations; no white-based gradients. Pure white is used only when the user explicitly requests it, never as the default recommendation.

**Light**

- **Rim light**: a clean, neutral-white rim light traces the product's smooth contours (preferred on a pure black background, **no blue tint**).
- **Soft even light**: clean and transparent with no harsh shadows (used only when the user explicitly wants pure white).
- **Top light + side backlight**: dramatically sculpted contours and silhouettes (pure black background).
- **Grazing light**: reveals material texture (extreme macro).

> **All light is neutral white, with no blue tint**; keep the tonality neutral (slightly warm is fine) and avoid an overall blue/cold-blue cast.

## 4. DNA Hard Rules (Non-Negotiable · Must Be Observed in Every Prompt)

 1. **With people / without people is the user's choice**: before starting, ask whether a model is needed (see Step 2). **With-people mode**—bind with `{{Mixed N}}` and let the user pick 1–3 people; the model is a **supporting accent** for temperament/mood, while the product is always the focal point and visual center (when they share the frame, focus and compositional weight stay on the product, shallow depth of field blurs the person, and the face/image never steals the show). **Without-people mode**—no person appears at all (for some products, human interaction actually feels odd); the whole video is carried by product hero shots / dark-field reveals / macro material shots / half-circle orbits.
 2. **Hook from the opening + every shot is an effective shot (hard constraint)**: **the opening rhythm must be fast, with the product appearing in the very first frame** (no empty shots/environmental setup followed by a slow build-up); **every single shot must be an effective shot**—serving to showcase the product or advance the pacing; **filler shots, meaningless transitions, purely atmospheric empty shots, and drawn-out lingering are forbidden**; when shots multiply, cut fast and waste not a single second.
 3. **★ Material is the lifeline of a premium look (highest priority · hard constraint)**: **"ugly/cheap" is, in the vast majority of cases, down to plasticky materials, fake highlights, and a CGI plastic look.** Every prompt **must spell out the product's specific material and its true optical behavior**, rather than a vague line like "premium texture":
    - Metal = brushed aluminum / anodized / matte or polished metal + **true highlight falloff and anisotropic reflection**;
    - Glass = polished glass + **Fresnel reflections, clear environmental reflections (e.g., HDRI reflections), and true refraction**;
    - Ceramic/plastic = matte ceramic glaze / soft-touch coating, **avoiding a cheap glossy-plastic feel**;
    - Silicone/fabric = true subsurface scattering / woven texture.\
      Fixed phrase: `photorealistic PBR materials, physically-based rendering, true-to-life reflections, realistic fresnel and specular falloff, fine micro-surface texture, no plastic-looking material, no fake highlights, no CGI plastic look`. **Get the material right and the video will never look cheap—this rule outranks camera movement, lighting effects, and composition.**
 4. **Dramatic lighting serves the material**: clean neutral-white rim light / top light + side backlight / soft even light; **the primary purpose of lighting is to make the material's reflections, texture, and contours visible** (true-to-life reflections &gt; flashy effects); shallow depth of field, film-grade, 4K ultra HD. **Light is neutral white, with no blue tint.**
 5. **Smooth cinematic camera movement (hard constraint)**: lead with smooth slow-motion camera moves—continuous, fluid motion with a cinematic breathing quality, **absolutely no frame jumps**. **① Orbit at most half a circle (≤180°); orbiting a full circle around the product is forbidden; ② push-ins and pull-backs are never in the same shot—one shot either pushes in or pulls out, never both (if the direction must change, cut to the next shot); ③ facial close-ups capture expression (with-people mode only).** No abrupt stutters / shake / whip pans.
 6. **No 3D teardown (hard constraint)**: no 3D exploded views, internal cutaways/see-throughs, fly-throughs into the product's interior, or other teardown shots. The product is showcased through its exterior material, light and shadow, and usage actions.
 7. **Color (hard constraint)**: natural and true-to-life, cinematic grade—**never an over-warm filter, and never an overall blue/cold-blue cast**; keep the tonality neutral (slightly warm is fine), avoid blue color casts; light is neutral white (true-to-life color, neutral grade, no blue cast, no over-warm filter).
 8. **Conceptual light effects off by default (prone to ugliness · hard constraint)**: glowing light trails / particle rings / flowing light bands—effects like these **are extremely easily generated by AI into cheap kitsch that drags down the quality—by default, never add them**. A premium tech feel should come from **clean lighting ratios + real material reflections**, not a screen full of glowing particles. **Only** when the product's own concept strongly requires it (e.g., audio products visualizing spatial audio/noise cancellation) **and the user explicitly asks for it** may you use one **with extreme restraint** (a single clean, elegant, restrained light-effect accent—never covering the frame, never in multiple places); otherwise, better to skip it.
 9. **Minimalist negative space + minimalist captions**: clean composition, deliberate negative space, one message per shot; product detail shots should be rich (macro/material/flyover) and cover every facet, **but don't keep ramming the logo into frame**; captions are only SF Pro / Helvetica minimalist callouts—few words, plenty of whitespace.
10. **Video prompt audio rule (mandatory)**: every Seedance/video-generation prompt must be silent; the following must be appended verbatim at the end: `No background music. No sound of any kind.` The body must not mention whooshes, clicks, ambient sound, foley, notification tones, chimes, dialogue, voiceover, or any sound design.
11. **Closing lockup**: a silver / space gray **brand logo** (metallic finish, centered, swapped for the product's own logo) on a clean background + the product name fading in in SF type + generous negative space.
12. **Forbidden**: filler shots/ineffective empty shots, generating single shots one by one, 3D teardown/exploded views/cutaways, dissolve/crossfade transitions, over-warm or blue-tinted filters, cluttered compositions, plasticky materials, exaggerated bizarre expressions, cheap kitschy effects, cramming the frame.

## 5. Pacing Rules (By Duration · Always 15s per Generation Segment)

Videos are **always generated in 15s segments** (never as single shots generated one by one); each 15s segment may contain multiple shots, with the cutting delegated to the model to handle automatically.

- **15s (fast pace)**: 1 segment. The pacing must be fast—**rapid alternation between human actions and product details**, multiple shots with quick cuts and clear edit points. Hook the viewer within the first 3 seconds (product hero / dark-field reveal / usage action).
- **30s (flexible content arrangement)**: split into 2 × 15s segments, **content order is free**—no forced "people first, details later"; interleave creatively (people using the product, hero shots, and details arranged freely); **in with-people mode, the full process of the person using the product must be shown**. **The logo lockup appears only at the end of the last segment.**
- **60s (flexible content arrangement)**: split into 4 × 15s segments, **content order freely arranged** (no fixed order enforced), confirm segment by segment before continuing; in with-people mode, ensure the full usage process is included; **the logo lockup appears only at the end of the last segment.**

## 7. Vocabulary

- **Shot size**: face close-up / extreme macro / close-up / medium / wide clean shot.
- **Camera movement (hard constraints)**: one-way push-in (no pull-back) / one-way pull-out (a separate shot from the push-in) / slow-motion half orbit ≤180° / macro fly-through / steady tracking. **Forbidden**: full 360° orbit, push-then-pull in one shot, flying through into the interior / disassembly.
- **Lighting**: clean neutral-white rim light (no blue tint) / soft even light / top + side-backlight / raking light.
- **Materials (required in every prompt · the more specific, the less cheap-looking)**: photorealistic PBR materials / physically-based rendering / true-to-life reflections / realistic fresnel reflection / accurate specular falloff / anisotropic brushed metal / anodized aluminium / polished glass with clear environment reflections / matte ceramic glaze / soft-touch coating / woven mesh / subsurface scattering / fine micro-surface texture. **Anti-ugly banned words**: `no plastic-looking material, no fake highlights, no CGI plastic look, no cheap glossy plastic`.
- **Color**: true-to-life color / neutral grade / **no blue cast** / no over-warm filter.
- **Silence (video prompt)**: always append at the end of every video generation prompt: `Do not generate background music, no sounds of any kind.` Do not include ambient sounds, foley, alert tones, chimes, dialogue, or narration.
- **Post-production music (Step 6 only)**: reference the fast-paced, quirky, tight surf rock + electronic groove of SneakyFreak - Subway surf; instrumental only, no vocals, no lyrics, no singing, no spoken voice; reference only the rhythmic vibe, do not copy melodies, samples, or recognizable sections.
- **Cuts / transitions**: clean hard cut / 【hard cut】 / match cut / seamless morph (**dissolve/crossfade forbidden**).
- **Motion quality (fixed sentence, added at the end of every prompt)**: `smooth fluid cinematic camera motion, steady consistent frame rate, no stutter, no frame drops, no jitter, seamless continuous coherent movement with natural motion blur.` For orbit shots add `slow steady cinematic half orbit up to 180 degrees, never a full circle`; for push/pull shots state the **single direction** explicitly (`a single slow push-in` or `a single slow pull-out`, never push-then-pull in the same shot).

## Workflow

### Step 1 · User uploads product / provides reference

Identify the product type (check Section 1), form/material/selling points, key detail highlights, and whether `{{Mixed N}}` is needed. **At this stage only identify and organize; do not generate any images. Reverse-engineer the reference video**: extract frames first and read the specs, then produce the same style. One-line command for frame extraction:

```
ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vf fps=1 -q:v 2 frames/frame_%04d.jpg   # 1 frame per second; set fps=2~4 for dense storyboards
```

Read frame by frame: how model shots and detail shots are paired, which techniques are used, background and lighting, pacing (is 15s fast enough), cuts, and the final hold frame; fill the knobs back in accordingly.

### Step 2 · Confirm all advance-selectable options in one pass (complete before generating images)

Before generating product images, model images, clothing images, and background images, **collect everything that can be selected in advance in one pass**; do not ask slowly question by question, and do not ask follow-up questions while generating. Keep a "you decide for me" option for every item:

1. **Model & appearance (ask everything in the first question)**: with model / no model; if with model, immediately ask in the same question the number of models 1/2/3, gender, age range, nationality/ethnic appearance, vibe keywords, hairstyle/body-type preference, and clothing reference/clothing style. It is forbidden to ask only "whether a model appears" and then, after locking the option, follow up separately with "model appearance preference".
2. Product type, core selling points, shot combination, and whether conceptual light effects are allowed (default: no).
3. Duration 15s/30s/60s, aspect ratio 16:9/9:16 (video is always generated in 15s segments; no need to select a generation mode).
4. Background presets: pure black / black-orange gradient / black-red gradient / black-gold gradient / black-gray smoky; pure white is available only when the user explicitly requests it.
5. Slogan, product name, logo/end-card text, and whether a background reference image exists.
6. Camera movement preference: dark-scene macro light wipe / linear highlight sweep across materials / full-view reveal / multi-point linked close-ups / you decide for me.\
   After confirmation, output a "locked selection list"; all subsequent image and video prompts strictly follow the list. If the user chooses with-model mode but omits appearance details, first auto-complete them per the "you decide for me" from the same round into a natural, camera-friendly, everyday-casual, unexaggerated model setting; do not interrupt a second time to ask.

### Step 3 · Generate reference images and moodboard (per the locked list)

> **All reference images are output at 16:9**: product three-view, product detail three-view, clothing 3-look sheet, character three-view, and background mood image — all generated at 16:9 (use image2 for the background image; GPT Image recommended for the rest to preserve fidelity/consistency).

1. `{{Product three-view}}`: front / side / back.
2. `{{Product detail three-view}}`: 3 key visible details (earbuds = charging case / earbud / mesh grille housing; phone = camera / screen / port; watch = dial / crown / strap).
3. With-model mode: generate a clothing 3-look image based on the locked appearance; clothing leans everyday casual, relaxed and natural (basics, solid colors, knitwear/shirt/hoodie/denim, etc.); revealing/erotic/overly exposed strictly forbidden; after the user picks 1 of the 3 looks, generate a character three-view on a white background (face close-up | full-body front | full-body back).
4. Background image: expand from the locked background preset, describing only the background/environment with no product or people; use it to generate `{{Scene mood image}}`.
Along with the image, attach the concept keywords + the locked knob-settings list. **Only after the user confirms is the visual direction locked—only then move to the next step.** The moodboard is the gatekeeping checkpoint.

### Step 4 · Generate Video in 15s Units (Unified Segmentation)

**Always treat 15s as one generation segment; never generate shot by shot**: **15s = 1 segment; 30s = 2 × 15s segments; 60s = 4 × 15s segments**. Each 15s segment uses one continuous prompt; internal multi-shot editing is left to the model's automatic cutting—give intent and rhythm without locking down camera positions; **each segment's content order is freely arranged; do not force "person first, details after"**; **in people mode, be sure to include the complete process of the person using the product**; multi-segment runs continue via the last frame / the same `{{Mixed N}}`; confirm each segment before producing the next, keeping `{{Mixed N}}`, wardrobe, product, background, and lighting consistent, with seamless joins and no continuity breaks. **The logo end card goes only at the end of the last segment.**

### Step 5 · Output in Seedance 2.0 Format (Strictly per the Moodboard)

> Output strictly per the locked selection list from Step 2; do not swap the background, model, duration, or aspect ratio on the fly again. Each prompt = one 15s segment.

The first sentence of each prompt references the background image: `Use {{scene_mood_ref}} as the background atmosphere reference.` Then follow with **the base block + background/lighting/sentence block + shot breakdown**, and add the motion-quality sentence at the end. Key points:

- **Consistency (strong constraint)**: background=`{{scene_mood_ref}}`; model=`{{Mixed N}}` (face/hair/wardrobe consistent); product=`{{product_three_view}}`/`{{product_detail_three_view}}`; no drift from shot to shot. Between segments, **stagger the content/shot scale/rhythm**; don't let consecutive segments look alike.
- **★ Material (mandatory in every prompt · highest priority)**: spell out the product's specific materials and true reflections (see DNA 3 and the Section 7 material lexicon), e.g. "brushed anodized aluminum frame with true highlight falloff," "polished glass back panel with clear environmental reflections (Fresnel)," "matte ceramic glaze"; every prompt must carry the fixed anti-ugly banned words `photorealistic PBR, true-to-life reflections, no plastic-looking material, no fake highlights, no CGI plastic look`. **The first gate against "cheap/ugly" results.**
- **Human interaction (people mode · strong constraint)**: before writing any model shot, first derive realistic usage actions per Section 2-A "Action Plausibility Check," and spell out **correct grip/wearing/contact points + approach→operation→feedback/result + matching expression + gaze/body following the motion** (e.g. "puts on the headphones, closes eyes and nods to the rhythm," "pulls the door open, sits in, starts the ignition and pulls away," "raises the camera to frame, presses the shutter, then smiles at the playback"); **banned: standing stiffly just holding it, no motion and no expression, implausible usage**; the product remains the focal point.
- **Camera movement (strong constraint)**: orbits \*\*≤ half a circle\*\*, push-in and pull-out in **separate shots**, continuous with **no frame jumps**; detail segments follow "one-way push-in → one detail per shot → macro flyover"; **no disassembly / no entering the interior**.
