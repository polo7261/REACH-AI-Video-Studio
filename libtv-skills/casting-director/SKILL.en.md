## 1. Aesthetic Guidelines

### A. Look Positioning

**Refined Chinese manhua aesthetic**: Always write the subject as `Chinese` (not East Asian — it tends to skew Korean). Clear bone structure, camera-ready, tempered with a touch of realism; avoid an overly cartoonish, fake feel.

### B. Skin (Hard Rule)

Realistic yet fine and clean. Keywords: `smooth clean realistic skin with a refined natural finish, subtle fine texture and minimal barely-visible pores, looking real but polished like high-end editorial retouching, never plastic, never rough bumpy blemished`. Do not use `lots of visible pores` (pitted look), nor `flawless glass-skin` (plastic look). Target: `smooth but natural`.

### C. Four Anti-Cheapness Principles

① Realistic skin texture ② Soft volumetric lighting (highlights on the forehead/nose bridge/cheekbones + shadows along the sides of the face) ③ Warm, translucent tones with a subsurface glow ④ Realistic detail, not over-sharpened.

### D. Two Overarching Principles

- Cool-warm contrast = premium feel (cool-toned skin + warm-toned lips and eyes)
- De-labeling = universality (no logos, no strong era markers, restrained accessories)

### E. Pure White Background (Highest Hard Rule)

**All asset backgrounds must be pure white #ffffff**, with no exceptions under any circumstances. Positive: `plain seamless pure white #ffffff studio background`. Negative: `no grey, no off-white, no coloured or textured background`.

### F. Facial Features (Routed by Gender)

**Highest-level face-shape hard rule (all characters, villains included)**: The face's bone structure must be clear and sharp, harmonious and photogenic; the jawline clean and not saggy; not wide, not square, not fleshy; upper and lower face in harmony. **The face is driven by the "facial features" + "overall temperament" fields; clothing style must never intrude on the face (see Section 5)**.
