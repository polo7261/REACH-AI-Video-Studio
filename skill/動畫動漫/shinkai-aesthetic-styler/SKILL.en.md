# Makoto Shinkai Theatrical Anime Film Pipeline

> 🔴 **Please follow this SKILL strictly, step by step.** Version: 2.0.8

## Foundational DNA (unconditional, carried by every single shot)
1. 🔴 **Light is the protagonist (Makoto Shinkai's signature, most important)**: Every frame is sculpted by light — strong backlight and semi-backlight, the sun's lens flares, bloom, highlight reflections on glass, water, and metal, glowing dust particles floating in the air. The light source direction is clear and dramatic; the characters' hair and silhouettes are rimmed in gold by the light, **actively pursuing the backlight-bloom and flare signature look**. But the light is "beautiful enough to make the heart tremble," not "an overexposed ruined shot" — highlights may bloom, yet the subject's information remains readable.
2. 🔴 **Photorealistic-grade finely detailed anime backgrounds + sky takes a huge share**: Shinkai's backgrounds are "a reality more beautiful than reality" — hand-drawn, finely detailed theatrical-anime backgrounds where every brick and every wire is rendered in meticulous detail, with precise perspective and extremely high detail density. The sky is the eternal protagonist: towering cumulonimbus clouds, a zenith blue verging on purple, magic-hour orange-purple gradients, starry skies and the Milky Way, contrails and comets. Compositions often give the sky more than half the frame, with characters standing tiny between heaven and earth.
3. **Deep depth of field, heaven and earth readable**: Wide-angle extreme long shots are often used to place people between a vast sky and a city, with depth stretching to the horizon; in close-ups the background remains finely discernible (anime backgrounds do not blur into bokeh, at most a slight aerial perspective). The scale contrast between people and the world is itself narrative.
4. 🔴 **Emotional core: outward and poetic youthful feeling (Shinkai's soul, hold it tight)**: This is Shinkai's most essential quality — longing and pursuit between a boy and a girl, with distance, time, and fate lying between them. The emotion **is outward, surging, lyrical**: running, shouting, gazing up at the sky, searching for one person in a sea of people. Beneath flows meeting and missing, distance and bonds, each other untouchable as if in parallel timelines, and "the world is so beautiful, and I am thinking of you." ⚠️ It must never be made into restrained, detached, cold slice-of-life, nor into cheap, melodramatic idol drama — the difference lies entirely in "using the whole sky and light to hold the emotion": the emotion is grand, but upheld by exquisite aesthetics and poetry — magnificent without being awkward.
5. **Let the world resonate with the emotion**: clouds drifting as if in time-lapse, raindrops smashing into splashes, trains passing a railway crossing, a comet slashing across the night sky, light flickering with emotion. Weather and celestial phenomena are amplifiers of emotion; the world breathes together with the characters' hearts.

## Quality Tail Suffix (🔴 the full version is attached by default only to model cards and prop composites; not attached to moodboards — style is anchored by the sref seed; scene images attach only the lightweight style-stabilizing suffix from Workflow step 3, not this full version)
`Makoto Shinkai theatrical anime film quality, photorealistic-grade finely detailed hand-painted backgrounds with extremely high detail density, characters in clean cel-animation style with crisp linework, large clear eyes with highlights and sky reflections, strong backlight and rim light, lens flares, bloom halos, glowing dust in the air, sky takes a large share of the frame (cumulonimbus / magic-hour gradients / starry sky), highly saturated rich translucent colors (blue-cyan base, orange-purple-gold as emotional highlights), high contrast, shadows tinted blue-purple rather than dead black, deep depth of field, background and horizon finely discernible; no live-action photography, no 3D-render plastic look, no photorealistic photo texture, no dull low saturation, no flat lighting without light and shadow, no background blurred into bokeh, no rough low-frame-rate web animation`

Write into memory.

3. **Assets**: Characters with roles must produce white-background studio-shot character card finalization images (locking face/costume/hairstyle); all subsequent shots reference them via @CharacterName. Scene images are single empty shots — absolutely no split-screen. 🔴 **Scene image generation iron rules (paired with the moodboard's new anchoring logic)**:
   - **Model: libimage** (unchanged).
   - 🎨 **Every scene image must attach the moodboard style anchor as reference** (anchoring only color tone/light quality/texture, not composition, to unify the film's color palette) — the moodboard's style is already pinned down by the sref seed, and the scene images' style is **conveyed entirely through this reference image**.
   - **Main body: four description categories + lightweight style-stabilizing words**: color/lighting-category style words such as color tone/palette/saturation/tonality/contrast/shadow color are still not written (left to the moodboard anchor to unify), but every scene image prompt ends with a fixed **lightweight style-stabilizing tail phrase** (to guard against style drift that the reference image alone can't hold): `Makoto Shinkai theatrical anime style, refined hand-painted background texture, clean anime linework`; the negative (separate field) always includes: `live-action footage, photorealistic photo texture, plastic 3D-render look`. That is all — no additional style words.
   - **The main body retains the four description categories**: ① Weather and sky conditions (clear sky/rain/snow/clouds/dusk/starry sky) ② Scene content (space, objects, environmental details) ③ Composition (viewpoint, depth of field, share of frame, foreground/midground/background relationships) ④ Light atmosphere (light source direction, backlight/lens flare/bloom, the form of light in the air, objective description of light-and-dark relationships).

   Named props are consolidated into a single white-background prop composite image.
4. **Video generation**: scene image + character images (+ prop composite when named props are involved) serve as references to lock consistency; the storyboard is written into the prompt's timeline. 🔴 **Video generation does not attach the moodboard style anchor** (the moodboard is used only in the scene image generation stage; video references are only the scene image/character images/prop images).
5. **Music**: confirm the direction only after all video characters are finalized, then generate (default: mureka). 🔴 **Do not composite it into the video right after generation** — because the current agent tool has problems adding subtitles to videos that contain music, the music must be held in reserve; after step 7's subtitles are done, ask the user whether to composite.
6. **Merging**: only split into multiple clips when spanning over 15 seconds; run them serially, then stitch them together with the video merging capability.
7. **Post-production editing** (after merging, or when the user specifies; tool: agent.tool.edit_video; do not invoke on your own initiative if the user hasn't mentioned it):
   - **Subtitles** add_subtitle: white text with black outline; language follows the dialogue language; if the user specifies one, translate first, then add. When voice mode is atmosphere-only, the film has no dialogue and no narration — skip the subtitle step without asking.
   - **Transitions** transition: user mentions but doesn't specify the method → default to a straight hard cut (Shinkai-style editing favors hard cuts and match cuts); options: hard cut/fade in/fade out/fade to black; AI transitions are not included here. ⚠️ The dissolve/crossfade ban only constrains the video generation stage; after merging, transitions can be added normally.
   - **Frame extraction** extract_frames: the agent invokes it automatically to help judge transitions; the user may also request it proactively.
   - **Trimming** clip: second-level precision; execute after the user describes the content to be trimmed.
   - **Text overlays** text_overlay: film title/chapter title cards, cast lists, name lower-thirds; users can customize the text content and style.
8. **Music compositing (after subtitles)**: 🔴 once all subtitles are added, use a choice card to ask the user whether to composite the music generated in step 5 into the video (choice card: composite/not yet). After the user confirms, perform the music compositing, then wrap up and deliver the film. ⚠️ The order must never be reversed — it must be "subtitles first, music after," because the current tool has problems adding subtitles to videos that already contain music.

## Script Production (executed after the story is finalized and before asset generation)
- 🔴 **Voice mode is a hard constraint (selected at entry question ⑤; observed throughout the entire script and film)**:
  - **Atmosphere-only**: write no dialogue or narration whatsoever; emotion is carried entirely by on-screen performance, light, and ambient sound; the script contains only actions, expressions, gaze, and environmental events.
  - **Dialogue-only**: wherever communication occurs, **actual dialogue must be written out**; it may not be glossed over with action descriptions like "the two converse."
  - **Narration-only**: **actual narration text must be written out**; narration is off-screen voiceover and on-screen characters do not mouth words (to prevent the video model from mismatching silent lip movements to the wrong language).
  - **Dialogue + narration interwoven**: both coexist; each passage is marked as dialogue or narration.
- 🔴 **Dialogue landing iron rule (silent-film treatment forbidden)**: when the mode includes dialogue/narration, the script must genuinely write out the full text of lines and narration — dialogue is annotated with the speaker (@CharacterName: "line"), narration is annotated (Narration: "text"). A script containing only action descriptions with no actual text is deemed non-compliant and must be rewritten.
- **Dialogue tonality (this skill's aesthetics)**: short sentences, everyday language, generous negative space — poetry hidden in plain words; emotional intensity is held up by situation and light rather than delivered bluntly through dialogue; the important things are often left half-said, or said through weather and scenery instead. No preachy tone, no melodramatic straight-up confession style, no long-winded speeches.
- **Narration tonality (this skill's aesthetics)**: first-person inner-monologue lyricism (the voice of "the world is so beautiful, and I am thinking of you"), complementing the visuals rather than restating them — the visuals give the world's vastness, the narration gives the heart's closeness. Short sentences with breathing room and negative space.
- 🔴 **Character performance tone table (required for every character with a role)**: define 2-3 **personality-driven signature micro-gestures/habitual postures** for each character with a role (determined by that character's personality, e.g., clutching the hem of their clothes when nervous, gazing out the window when thinking, heels leaving the ground for half a beat when happy), archived alongside the script. **Consistent across shots throughout the film**: the @character action in each shot's video prompt draws preferentially from the tone table; signature actions are used preferentially as action-linking cut-point material (a habitual action begins → the next shot picks it up).
- **Script format**: scene numbering + location/time + characters present + scene content (actions and events) + voice content (full dialogue/narration written per mode). Each scene's landing point corresponds to one filmable life/emotion segment, to be drawn on directly by the storyboard timeline.

Main body in Chinese; technical terms (lens flare/god rays/bloom/cel shading) stay in English when necessary. Negative words are physically separated from the main body and placed in a dedicated negative field.

## Character Iron Rules
- **Lock the texture, free the identity**: the Makoto Shinkai theatrical cel-animation texture is a hard constraint; hair color/hairstyle/clothing/identity and demeanor respect the user's settings — no Shinkai-ification corrections are applied.
- **Performance tone consistent across shots**: each character with a role has 2-3 personality-driven signature micro-gestures/habitual postures defined at the script stage (see the "Script Production" chapter), consistent across all shots in the film; the character card locks only appearance, the performance tone is governed by the script, and video prompts draw from it.
- **The face is a clean, luminous anime face**: soft cel shading, clean linework, translucent skin tone leaning warm pink; **the eyes are the soul** — large and clear, richly layered irises, always with highlight points and ambient-light reflections (sky/sunset colors mirrored in the eyes). Never render a photorealistic human face, a 3D-rendered face, or a thick-paint oil-painting face.
- **Model sheet ≠ final footage**: the model sheet uses a pure white background + bright, even frontal lighting (face fully illuminated, facial details prioritized); **during the model-sheet stage, never use backlight/silhouette/lens flare** — blooming backlight is the signature of the final footage, not a look-lock requirement; the model sheet's job is to see the face clearly and lock it down. 16:9 layout: three views on the left (front/side/back) + large facial close-up on the right (mandatory, cannot be omitted, must not be drawn as full body).
- 🔴 **Model-sheet template (fill in as-is; do not change the structure or the texture tail wording; replace only the [] placeholders; if the layout falls apart / views are incomplete, fall back to the English version)**:
```
Animated character three-view look-lock sheet on a pure white seamless background, 16:9 horizontal layout, strictly divided into two areas that must both exist simultaneously. Left area: three full-body views of the same character — front/side/back — standing side by side, equal in height, feet aligned. Right area (must exist, cannot be omitted): a large facial close-up of the same character occupying most of the right area, clearly showing the face and upper chest, used to lock the face shape, gaze, hair color, and eye color — the right-area close-up is as important as the left-area three views and must be rendered. Makoto Shinkai theatrical-feature anime texture, applied to [character identity and temperament], bright soft even frontal light fully illuminating the face, facial linework crisp and clean, soft cel shading, translucent healthy skin tone (not gray or dim, not 3D plastic, not photorealistic), eyes large and clear with richly layered irises and highlight points, [body type], [hairstyle and hair color], [outfit], [expression and gaze], holding no props, bags, or accessories of any kind (character only; props get their own separate prop sheet), natural relaxed neutral standing pose, clean translucent colors keyed to [primary color 1] and [primary color 2] (saturated and vivid, not washed out, not muddy), theatrical-feature-level high-precision artwork, clean stable lines, bright even white-background look-lock style, high-detail animated face, clear facial features, all views and the close-up maintain the same character consistency
```
  Negative (separate box): `live-action photography, photorealistic photo face, 3D-rendered plastic look, thick-paint oil painting, backlight, silhouette, lens flare, overexposed bloom, dramatic lighting, dim low-saturation, dirty worn distressed look, broken linework, distorted facial features, backpack school bag held items accessories props, facial inconsistency, a different person, multiple different characters, two separate character cards, full body in the right area, missing facial close-up, complex background, watermark`
- 🔴 **Character images carry no props**: the model sheet renders only the character itself (figure/outfit/hair/face), never carrying a backpack / holding a phone, umbrella, or books / wearing non-essential accessories — props are managed uniformly by the prop composite sheet and referenced again in later shots (to prevent conflict between props bundled into character images and the prop sheet). Clothing worn on the body doesn't count as a prop; add "backpack, school bag, held items, accessories, props" to the negatives.
- **Child safety**: forbid the combo of "minor age numbers + body-measurement words + revealing clothing"; switch to school-age temperament words. Shinkai's works mostly star middle school / high school protagonists, so this rule must be observed with special strictness; for settings like school uniforms, use neutral descriptions such as "student temperament, school uniform".

## Scene cards (draw on per scene; the style is already pinned down by sref→moodboard; the cards only supply material elements — four categories: weather/scene/composition/lighting — just combine them when writing the prompt)
- ☁️ **Sky/cloud sea**: cumulonimbus stacked like mountains / starry Milky Way / a comet's contrail; gold rims on backlit cloud edges; wide angle, sky taking up more than half the frame, the character tiny against the horizon. Whenever the user mentions sky/clouds/comet/starry sky, apply this card unconditionally.
- 🌆 **Magic hour dusk (signature)**: around sunset, clouds ignited by the setting sun; warm golden backlight, lens flare, long shadows trailing across the ground, glass reflecting the sunset; characters steeped in backlight, golden rim light on their silhouettes at full strength.
- 🏙️ **City/trains/railroad crossings**: utility poles and wires, light streaming across train windows, red lights at railroad crossings, the glow of convenience stores and vending machines; background detail density maxed out; characters moving through or waiting within the large scene.
- 🌧️ **Rain/after rain**: visible raindrops, ripples across puddles, water hanging on umbrellas and glass; reflective road surfaces, headlights stretched into streaks; after the rain, light breaks through the clouds, wet streets shimmering with reflections.
- 🏫 **Campus/everyday lyricism**: classroom windows, corridors and rooftops, cherry blossoms or summer green; window light slicing in slantwise, dust drifting in the window light, curtains lifted by the wind.
- 🌌 **Night/starry sky**: the Milky Way and city lights gazing at each other; moonlight, shooting stars, blooming city glow; characters looking up from rooftops/hillsides/bridges, deep depth of field.

## Camera movement (one type per shot, meshed with the emotion, dynamic impact by default)
Fast sprint tracking (hugging tight on a chase run) / rapid push-ins and pull-outs (pushing into the eyes or pulling out to reveal heaven and earth) / orbiting the character / low-altitude dives through the scene (FPV-style sweeps over streets/clouds/rail tracks) / whip pans bridging actions / large fast pans sweeping across sky and city / crane rising dramatically to reveal the scale of heaven and earth / slight camera shake on impact (landing / door bursting open / train rushing past). ⚙️ Dynamic camera moves are usable across all segments, not limited to climaxes; slow push-ins and locked extreme wides are reserved as accents for gazing moments (at most 1-2 shots per 15-second segment); all other shots must keep moving. Shinkai's camera is "a lyric poem running with the world" — prefer surge over stillness.

## Editing rhythm (lyrical rise and fall, cuts riding on action continuity)
- 🔴 **Shot-count target: 5-6 storyboard shots per 15 seconds** (~2-3 seconds each); emotional rise and fall is conveyed through in-shot camera movement and action density, not by stretching individual shots — gazing segments get longer shots (3-second tier), climax segments get short shots (2-second tier), but the total shot count holds at 5-6.
- 🔴 **Cut logic: action continuity (match cut)**: every hard cut must mesh with the actions on both sides of the cut — cut out when the previous shot's action reaches its apex or turning point, and start the next shot from the continuation of that action or an echoing action (e.g., a gaze tilting up toward the sky → cut to an overhead of the sky; a hand reaching for the door handle → cut to the door being pushed open; a running foot striking the ground → cut to a wide of the figure sprinting). **Cuts are decided by action, not by elapsed time.**
- Scenic empty shots are body text, not transitions: empty shots of sky, clouds, trains, and street corners carry emotion; they can stand as shots in their own right and intercut with character shots via action/gaze linkage.
- Shot scale can jump drastically: cutting straight from an extreme wide (a person tiny within heaven and earth) to an eye close-up is common Shinkai grammar, exempt from the "one-step" restriction, but the jump must be driven by action continuity, not arbitrary.
- Within each shot, actions play out in full and cuts land on action beats — no meaningless fragment cutting.

## Music (mureka by default; may include vocal theme-song segments)
- Temperament: lyrical, surging, with clear emotional progression and climactic release — music and picture are each other's engine (RADWIMPS-style film-music unity); at the climax the music may charge to the emotional forefront.
- 🎹 **Piece 1 · Piano lyrical main line** (piano-led + strings-driven), three sub-branches:
  - A · Everyday life and heart-flutter: bright, brisk piano motifs with strings lightly underscoring — the transparency of youth. For campus/everyday life/encounters.
  - B · Longing and distance: slow piano, long melodies tinged with wistfulness, strings drifting in, rising again after silence. For separation/gazing at the sky/memories.
  - C · Running and searching (climax): piano + strings + rhythm section advancing layer by layer, emotion ratcheting up to eruption, paired with fast-cut montage. For running/searching/time-space interweaving/confessions.
- 🌠 **Piece 2 · Grand celestial phenomena**: vast textures laid by strings and synthesizers, carrying a sense of the sacred and the fated, slowly building to grandeur. For comets/starry skies/supernatural phenomena/world-scale vistas.
- 🎤 **Piece 3 · Theme-song vocal segment (the only vocals allowed)**: pop-rock arrangement + vocal melody, for the film's emotional peak or the ending — enabled only after user confirmation (ask via a choice card: enable / don't enable); by default the first two pieces stay purely instrumental. ⚠️ Score vocals belong to the music layer and neither constrain nor are constrained by the script's "vocal mode" — a film in pure-ambience mode can still use the theme-song vocal segment.
- **Piece selection**: campus everyday encounter → A; longing/gazing/memories → B; running/searching/climax → C; celestial spectacle → Piece 2; film's highest point/ending → ask the user whether to bring in Piece 3. Multiple pieces may coexist in one film, switching with the segments; there must be an overall emotional arc of "build-develop-erupt-settle".
- No flat, ambient-noise-style scoring running through the whole film; no total lack of rise and fall from start to finish. Shinkai's music must "push the emotion forward," and the climax must deliver.

## Video Iron Rules
- 🔴 **Multiple shots per single clip (mandatory by default)**: seedance runs at most 15 seconds per generation and natively supports multiple shots within one clip + hard cuts between shots—**do not follow the "one shot per clip + post-edit splicing" approach**. 🔴 **Shot count target: 5-6 shots per 15 seconds, about 2-3 seconds per shot** (gazing segments may go to the 3-second tier, climax segments to the 2-second tier; keep the total shot count at 5-6); shorter clips under 15 seconds are converted at the same density (8 seconds ≈ 3 shots / 10-12 seconds ≈ 4-5 shots). **Only split into multiple clips when exceeding 15 seconds; serialize them and then splice using the video compositing capability**. The prompt advances second by second; each shot = shot size + @character action + camera movement + environment + **the action-bridging cut point to the next shot**, with hard cuts explicitly breaking between shots.
- 🔴 **Absolutely forbidden to generate background music (iron rule, no exceptions)**: during the video generation stage, audio **allows only ambient sound + dialogue**; any form of background music, score, melody, humming, or drum beats is strictly forbidden—even when the shot's emotion is intense, even when instruments or headphones appear in the frame, even if the model tends to auto-score, it is absolutely not allowed. BGM is only layered in at the step 8 compositing stage. Makoto Shinkai-style ambient sound (rain / passing trams / cicadas / wind / city background hum / railroad-crossing warning bells) is extremely important; every video prompt carries the **standing declaration (write it whether or not this clip has dialogue, without omitting a single word)**: "Audio keeps only rich, realistic ambient sound and dialogue; absolutely no background music, no score, no melody, no humming, no beats or drum rhythms"; the negatives **always include**: "background music, score, BGM, melody, humming, beats and drum rhythms, music, background music, score, soundtrack, melody, humming". After producing the prompt, self-check: are both the declaration and the negatives present (if either is missing, rewrite).
- 🔴 **Language lock + voice mode lock**: every video prompt carries the standing declaration "All character dialogue/narration is in [selected language]; even when the model fills in lines on its own, use only this language; lines/subtitles/lip-sync in other languages are forbidden" (write it whether or not this shot has dialogue); negatives add "lines in other languages, mixed-language lip-sync". This guards against the wrong language popping up when the model adds lines to dialogue-free shots on its own. Also append according to voice mode: ambient-only → standing declaration "No dialogue or narration throughout the film; characters do not speak or open their mouths", negatives add "dialogue, narration, speaking lip-sync"; narration-only → standing declaration "Characters do not open their mouths" (narration is off-screen voice-over, handled at the compositing stage); pure dialogue/interleaved → write dialogue lines into the corresponding storyboard shots per the script.
- **Character consistency via reference images + actions must have explosive force**: character texture and style are conveyed by the attached character/scene image references; the prompt body contains no style words; the body constrains action and stability—**max out the action amplitude**: full-speed sprinting, leaping and vaulting, sudden stops and sharp turns, climbing and scrambling over, ducking low and bursting through doorways, hair and hems whipping and fluttering dramatically, footsteps kicking up splashes or fallen leaves and dust; even in static shots the character must show a clear action in progress (turning the head / reaching out / getting up), with faces not breaking down. Standing negatives: live-action conversion, 3D conversion, broken linework, facial-feature distortion, stiff posing, standing frozen in place.
- **No dissolves/cross-fades**: hard cuts between shots; standing negatives: no dissolve/cross-fade/fade.
- **Light and shadow must be present**: every shot in the video must have a clear light source and light effects (backlight/reflections/lamplight), never flat lighting. Fast camera moves and rapid light changes (light flares sweeping across the face, passing through light and shadow, light streaking past car windows) may be used throughout and are encouraged.
