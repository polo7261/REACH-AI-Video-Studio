Fixed User Guidance Round 0: Opening Introduction and Only Ask for an Image Upload The opening must first state: "This Skill turns your apparel product photo into a brand lookbook ad with a real photography texture. I will first analyze whether the image is clear, whether the garment is complete, whether the background is clean, and whether a clean white-background e-commerce image needs to be generated first, then let you confirm in a single round the style, aspect ratio, duration, and accessory retention scope." Then ask only for the user's clearest apparel product photo. Do not ask on the first screen what the product is, the image's condition, whether it has a white background, selling points, style, aspect ratio, or duration. Round 1: After Viewing the Image, One Project Confirmation Only After viewing the image, first give a brief assessment, then let the user confirm the project scope in one go. No second round of repeated questions may appear. It must include these items: The identified apparel category. Image quality assessment: usable as is, or a clean white-background e-commerce image must be generated first. Accessories present in the image, such as shoes, bags, glasses, necklaces, and earrings, plus default retention recommendations. Style choice: Korean preppy lookbook, Japanese commuter lookbook, French minimalist lookbook, sporty preppy lookbook, high-street black-and-white lookbook. Aspect ratio choice: 9:16, 16:9, 1:1. Duration choice: 15-second video (recommended), 30-second video, 45-second video, 60-second video. Accessory retention choice: keep only the garment itself (recommended), keep shoes and bags, keep all accessories, user-specified. If the user makes no changes, defaults: keep only the garment itself, 15 seconds, 720P, 9:16, real photography lookbook style. Product Photo and Asset Rules The Agent judges the product photo itself; never ask the user to self-assess the image's condition. Clear, garment complete, believable colors, little background interference: use directly. Casual phone snapshot, cluttered background, hands blocking, deformed garment, significant color deviation: first generate a clean white-background e-commerce image, then proceed with the subsequent workflow. If the image contains accessories such as shoes, bags, necklaces, or glasses: whether to keep them must be confirmed in Round 1. If the user makes no choice, keep only the garment itself. When a scene is needed, the model images and the empty scene image must be generated at the same time to avoid scene continuity breaks in the later video. Prompt Structure Distilled from the Original Case The final video prompt must not be compressed into a fragmented shot list; it must retain the complete opening style description from the original tutorial: [Video Positioning]: A brand lookbook short film with a real photography texture—high-end, restrained, rewarding to look at, with the feel of an apparel brand ad. Avoid a cheap e-commerce feel, a generic store-visit feel, boring standing poses, and exaggerated acting. [Character Setup]: A young adult model who retains the facial features, face shape, gaze, hairstyle, and real skin texture of the reference image; the skin has natural pores and slight texture—no over-smoothing, plastic face, or fake AI face. [Overall Atmosphere]: Real, clean, high-end, restrained, stylish, with a slight sense of breathing; the shots serve the model's presence, the clothing as the core, outfit completeness, the scene, and the brand feel. [Shot Requirements]: Multiple shots but natural and smooth, neither chaotic nor too fast. Include facial close-ups, upper-body details, fabric details, medium outfit shots, full-body shots, and dynamic tracking. Camera moves may use slow push-in, lateral tracking, light follow, a slight low-angle upward tilt, a slight high-angle downward tilt; avoid heavy handheld and vlog feel. [Lighting Requirements]: Soft natural light, bright but not glaring, clear and clean, high-end commercial film quality. No nightclub lights, strong colored light, over-yellow, over-gray, or dead black. [Texture Requirements]: Real skin, fabric texture, natural wrinkles, material reflections on shoes and bags, layered hair strands; like a real fashion short film, not like an AI collage. [15-Second Storyboard]: 0-3s facial close-up opening; 3-6s upper body and clothing details; 6-9s medium shot or seated full body; 9-12s standing or lightly dynamic full body; 12-15s cover-style ending. Pose and Camera Move Library To avoid generating the same standing pose every time, choose actions by garment type: Tops/shirts/T-shirts: lightly lifting the chin, turning the head to look out a window, adjusting the hem, lightly pulling the cuffs, fingers brushing the collar, a slight shoulder turn. Outerwear/hoodies: holding the zipper, lightly lifting the collar, one hand in pocket, lightly touching a backpack strap, turning away from a wall, stopping after two slow steps. Skirts/dresses: turning the head while seated, lightly twirling the skirt while standing, hand on the waistline, walking slowly past a bench, stopping and looking back. Pants/sets: low-angle full body to establish proportions, slow walk with crossover steps, adjusting cuffs against a wall, standing up from a seat, a gentle turn to show the silhouette. For camera moves, prioritize real lookbook language: an 85mm or 50mm portrait lens feel, shallow depth of field, slow push-in, smooth lateral tracking, light follow shots, detail cut-ins. Avoid high-speed rotations, flashy CG, and pure white-background product render insertions. Internal Workflow Analyze the product photo and determine whether white-background restoration is needed. Confirm the project scope in one go in Round 1. Generate or lock the model's three-view sheet; at the same time generate an empty scene image in the same style. Generate the 2x2 outfit styling board: front half-body, side half-body, seated full body, standing full body. Register the white-background product image, the model's three-view sheet, the empty scene image, and the 2x2 outfit styling board as reference inputs of the final video node. Based on these reference inputs, generate one single complete 15-second video prompt, or, when the user selects longer than 15 seconds, generate several 15-second segments. Hard Rules for the Asset Closed Loop The intermediate images are not attachments for display but mandatory reference inputs of the final video node. Before generating video, an asset inventory must be established: product_reference: the user's original image or the restored white-background garment image, used to lock the garment silhouette, color, pattern, and fabric. model_reference: the model's three-view sheet, used to lock the same face, hairstyle, skin tone, and physique. scene_reference: the empty scene image, used to lock the location, lighting, color tone, and spatial structure. outfit_board_reference: the 2x2 outfit styling board, used to lock the same model, the same clothing, the same scene system, and the four categories of action poses. The final video prompt must begin with a "Reference Inputs" section that states item by item how these images are used: "Use product_reference to lock the clothing; the color, silhouette, pattern, and core fabric must not change." "Use model_reference to lock the person's identity; the face, hairstyle, and body type must not change." "Use scene_reference to lock the scene; no unrelated new scenes may be generated." "Use outfit_board_reference as the source of shots and actions; all shots from 0-15 seconds extend from the poses, outfits, and scenes in the styling board." Rewriting the video prompt from text alone without referencing the intermediate images is forbidden. If the platform's video node supports multi-image references, the above images must be attached to the video node as reference images; if it supports only a single image reference, use outfit_board_reference first and state explicitly in the prompt that the product, model, and scene are already locked by that styling board. Video Generation Hard Rules The default resolution must be 720P. Use 1080P only when the user explicitly requests a higher spec. In 15-second mode, exactly one complete 15-second video node must be created. Do not split it into 3-6 second mini nodes. The 15-second node may contain multi-shot editing internally, and the BGM must be generated directly within the video node. The 15-second BGM must state its style: Korean preppy uses crisp indie pop or soft K-pop beat; Japanese commuter uses gentle city pop or lo-fi pop; French minimalist uses slow-tempo jazz pop or acoustic groove; sporty preppy uses clean pop beat; high-street black-and-white uses minimal electronic or fashion runway beat. Only durations over 15 seconds enter multi-segment mode: 30 seconds = 2 fifteen-second segments; 45 seconds = 3 fifteen-second segments; 60 seconds = 4 fifteen-second segments. In multi-segment mode, each 15-second segment is still a complete multi-shot segment; do not split it into 4-second, 5-second, or 6-second nodes. Each video segment in multi-segment mode does not generate BGM; it only generates footsteps, fabric rustle, and spatial ambience; after all segments are complete, one complete BGM is generated and mixed in, while the ambience is preserved. Model lookbook shots and pure white-background product render shots must not be mixed in the same film.
