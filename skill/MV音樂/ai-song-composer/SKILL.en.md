# songwriting-and-ai-music

Read this skill when the user wants to write lyrics, compose music, or generate songs/theme songs/music with vocals. The final music is generated by `generate_audio` (music); this skill covers two things: the craft of songwriting itself, and how to write musical intent as a music prompt that the generation engine can execute correctly.

Everything below is guidance, not iron rules. Art deliberately breaks rules by nature — serve the song, not the checklist itself; any rule that doesn't serve this song, throw it out.

## 1. Song Structure (Pick One or Invent Your Own)

Common skeletons; mix them, modify them, or discard them entirely:

```
ABABCB  Verse/Chorus/Verse/Chorus/Bridge/Chorus     (most pop/rock)
AABA    Verse/Verse/Bridge/Verse (chorus-type refrain)   (jazz standards, ballads)
ABAB    Verse/Chorus alternating                    (simple and direct)
AAA     Verse/Verse/Verse (no chorus, narrative)    (folk, storytelling)
```

The six building blocks:
- **Intro** — sets the tone, pulls the listener in
- **Verse** — story, details, world-building
- **Pre-Chorus** — optional; the tension ramp before the chorus
- **Chorus** — the emotional core, the part that gets remembered
- **Bridge** — one detour; a shift in perspective or tonality
- **Outro** — the farewell; can echo or subvert what came before

You don't need all six. Some great songs are a single section of material that keeps evolving. **Structure serves the emotion, not the other way around.**

## 2. Rhyme, Meter, and Sound

**Rhyme types (tight to loose):**
- Perfect rhyme: lean/mean
- Family rhyme: crate/braid
- Assonance: had/glass (same vowel, different ending)
- Consonance: scene/when (different vowels, similar endings)
- Near/slant rhyme: enough to suggest a connection without locking it in

Mixing is best: all perfect rhymes can sound like a nursery rhyme; all slant rhymes can feel half-hearted. The blend is where it comes alive.

**Internal rhyme**: don't just rhyme line endings; rhyme within lines too. "We pruned the lies from bleeding trees / Distilled the storm from entropy" — "lies/flies," "trees/entropy" create echoes within the line.

**Meter**: the rhythm of stressed and unstressed syllables.
- Matching syllable counts between parallel lines helps singability
- **Stress placement matters more than total syllable count**
- Read it aloud — wherever you stumble is where the meter needs fixing
- Deliberately breaking meter can create emphasis or surprise

## 3. The Emotional Dynamics Arc

Treat a song as a journey, not a flat road.

**Energy map (rough reference, not hard rules):**
```
Intro: 2-3  |  Verse: 5-6  |  Pre-Chorus: 7
Chorus: 8-9  |  Bridge: as needed  |  Final Chorus: 9-10
```

The most powerful dynamic device is **contrast**:
- Whisper before you scream — it hits harder than screaming all the way through
- Sparse before dense; slow before fast; low before high
- A "landing" only works because of the build-up before it
- **Silence is itself an instrument**

"Whisper → roar → whisper": start intimate, push to full power, then pull back to vulnerability. Works for ballads, epics, and anthems.

## 4. The Craft of Lyric Writing

**Show, don't tell (most of the time):**
- "I'm sad" = flat
- "Your hoodie still hanging on the hook by the door" = alive
- But sometimes the direct statement "I gave my life" is itself where the power lies — not an iron rule

**Hook (the memorable bit):**
- The line that gets remembered, hummed, and repeated
- Usually the title or the core phrase
- Works best when melody + lyrics + emotion all align
- Place it where it should land hardest (often the first or last line of the chorus)

**Prosody (lyrics and music working together):**
- Stable emotions (resolution, calm) pair with resolved melodies, perfect rhymes, and resolved chords
- Unstable emotions (longing, doubt) pair with wandering melodies, slant rhymes, and unresolved chords
- Verse melodies usually sit low and the chorus climbs — but flip it if it serves the song

**What to avoid (unless you're doing it on purpose):**
- Autopilot clichés (dropping "heart of gold" with no setup)
- Warping word order just to force a rhyme ("Yoda-style" inversions)
- The same energy in every section (flat dynamics)
- Treating the first draft as gospel — revision is part of the writing itself

## 5. Parody and Adaptation

When rewriting an existing song with new lyrics:

**Draw the skeleton first**: map the original's structure —
- Count the syllables in each line
- Mark the rhyme scheme (ABAB, AABB, etc.)
- Identify which syllables are **stressed**
- Note where the long notes/sustains fall

**Fit new lyrics to the skeleton:**
- Align stressed syllables with the same beats as the original
- Total syllable count can float by 1-2 unstressed syllables
- On sustained notes, match the original's **vowel sound** wherever possible (if the original sustains the "oo" in "LOOOVE", "FOOOD" fits better than "LIFE")
- Single-syllable word swaps are the cleanest way to preserve rhythm (Crime → Code, Snake → Noose)
- Sing the new lyrics over the original melody — wherever you stumble, revise

**Concept:**
- Pick a concept strong enough to carry the whole song
- Build outward from the title/hook
- Generate lots of raw material first (puns, phrases, imagery), then pick the best to fill the structure
- If a specific line must land in a particular spot, work backwards from the rhyme scheme to build the lines before it

**Keep a few original lines**: leaving a few lines or structural moments from the original fully intact adds recognizability and lets the listener feel the connection to the source.

## 6. Writing the Music Prompt (handed to `generate_audio` · music generation)

### Style/Genre Description Field

**Formula (adjust as needed):** genre + mood + era + instruments + vocal characteristics + production texture + dynamics

```
Bad:   "a sad rock song"
Good:  "Cinematic orchestral spy thriller, 1960s Cold War era, smoky sultry female vocals,
        big band jazz, brass section (trumpet + French horn), sweeping strings, minor key,
        warm vintage analog texture"
```

**Describe the "journey," not just a list of genres:**
```
"Opens with ghostly whispers and sparse piano. Muted brass layers in gradually.
 Pushes to full orchestration at the chorus. The second verse erupts in a raw, roaring belt.
 The outro pulls back to a lone piano and fragile whispers, fading into silence."
```

**Key points:**
- The style field can run long — spend the budget describing the "journey," not just stacking genre words
- **No famous musicians' names or trademarks** — describe the sound itself rather than referencing works/artists
  - Say "1960s Cold War spy-thriller brass" rather than a specific film composer's style name
  - Say "90s grunge" rather than a specific band name
- When you have preferences, state BPM and key explicitly
- Use "exclude styles"-type descriptions to say what you **don't** want
- Counterintuitive genre pairings often shine: "bossa nova + trap", "Appalachian gothic", "chiptune jazz"
- Build a **vocal persona**, not just a gender: "a weathered lounge singer, smoky voice with a rasp, starting fragile and building toward a devastating belt"

### Metatags (in square brackets within the lyrics field `[tag]`)

**Structural:**
`[Intro] [Verse] [Verse 1] [Pre-Chorus] [Chorus] [Post-Chorus] [Hook] [Bridge] [Interlude] [Instrumental] [Instrumental Break] [Guitar Solo] [Breakdown] [Build-up] [Outro] [Silence] [End]`

**Vocal performance:**
`[Whispered] [Spoken Word] [Belted] [Falsetto] [Powerful] [Soulful] [Raspy] [Breathy] [Smooth] [Gritty] [Staccato] [Legato] [Vibrato] [Melismatic] [Harmonies] [Choir] [Harmonized Chorus]`

**Dynamics:**
`[High Energy] [Low Energy] [Building Energy] [Explosive] [Emotional Climax] [Gradual swell] [Orchestral swell] [Quiet arrangement] [Falling tension] [Slow Down]`

**Gender:**
`[Female Vocals] [Male Vocals]`

**Atmosphere:**
`[Melancholic] [Euphoric] [Nostalgic] [Aggressive] [Dreamy] [Intimate] [Dark Atmosphere]`

**Sound effects:**
`[Vinyl Crackle] [Rain] [Applause] [Static] [Thunder]`

**Usage rules:**
- Put tags in both the style field and the lyrics field — they reinforce each other
- At most 5-8 tags per section — too many confuses the engine
- Don't contradict yourself (don't use both `[Calm]` and `[Aggressive]` in the same section)

### The Split Style/Lyrics Creation Mode

- For real work, always use the "separate Style + Lyrics" mode (not the single-column simple mode)
- Plan within the lyrics field's limit of roughly ~3,000 characters (about 40-60 lines)
- Always add structural tags — without them the engine defaults to a flat Verse/Chorus/Verse with no emotional arc

## 7. Phonetic Tricks for AI Singers

AI vocal models don't "read" words — they "pronounce" them, so guide them deliberately:

**Spell phonetically:**
- Respell words the way they actually sound (e.g., "through" → "thru")
- Proper nouns fail most often — always test them in a short snippet beforehand
- Force correct pronunciation with near-homophones (e.g., swap a frequently mispronounced word for a same-sounding but easier-to-read spelling)
- Use hyphens to steer syllable breaks (e.g., "Re-search", "bio-engineering")

**Performance control:**
- ALL CAPS = louder, more intense
- Stretched vowels ("lo-o-o-ove") = sustains/melisma
- Ellipses ("I... need... you") = dramatic pauses
- Hyphenated stretching ("ne-e-ed") = emotional stretching

**Always remember:**
- Spell numbers out ("24/7" → "twenty four seven")
- Separate acronym letters with spaces ("AI" → "A I" or "A-I")
- Test proper nouns/uncommon words separately first with a ~30-second short clip
- **Once generated, pronunciation is locked in** — to change it, edit the lyric text and regenerate, rather than patching afterwards

## 8. Creative Workflow

1. Write the concept/hook first — what is this song's emotional core?
2. If it's an adaptation, map the original's structure first (syllables, rhyme scheme, stress)
3. Brainstorm raw material freely — no structure yet
4. Fit the lyrics into a structural draft
5. Speak/sing it aloud — catch the stumbles, fix the meter
6. Write the style description (Suno-style) — chart the dynamic journey
7. Add metatags to the lyrics for performance guidance
8. Generate at least 3-5 versions — treat them like recording takes
9. Pick the best one and use the extend/continuation feature to keep building on promising sections
10. If a stroke of genius appears by accident, keep it

**Set expectations**: roughly one satisfying result per 3-5 generations. Iterating is the norm. Style drifts during extension — restate the genre/mood each time you extend.

## 9. Lessons Learned

- Describing the **dynamic arc** in the style field ("whisper → roar → whisper") matters far more than stacking genre words — it's effectively a "performance map" for the engine.
- In parodies, keeping a few original lines adds recognizability and emotional weight — the listener can feel the original's "ghost" is still there.
- The bridge is the best spot to swap imagery: replace the original's concrete references with metaphors for your theme while keeping their emotional function (reflection/turn/epiphany).
- Single-syllable swaps in the hook/tagline are the cleanest way to keep the rhythm while changing the meaning.
- A strong vocal persona description sways the result more than any single metatag.
- Don't get attached to the rules themselves. If a line breaks the meter but moves people, keep it — **the feeling is the substance; craft serves art, not the other way around**.
