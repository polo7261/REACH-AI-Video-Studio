# idol-mv

Use this skill when creating Heisei-era AKB48-style youth idol MVs. Core temperament: **Japanese-style film texture + documentary handheld cinematography + campus youth narrative + girl-group ensemble feel**, with the 2009–2014 golden era as the aesthetic baseline. Core baseline: all designs align with the "Heisei-era AKB48 golden-age youth idol MV"—**not perfect idols, but ordinary members who are shining right now**.

> This environment's adaptation: Seedance/MultiModalToVideo → `generate_video_v3`; element images/TextToImage/ImageToImage → `generate_image_v3`; BGM/VO/text_to_instrumental/text_to_narration → `generate_audio`; text_editor → `write_file_v2`; video_assembler → `composite_video`; `<<<image_n>>>`/asset_id slots → `reference_nodes`+`{{NodeKey}}`; milestone confirmation → `ask_human`. **Prompt writing conventions and camera-movement vocabulary are fully inlined in this skill, not outsourced to image-prompt-director / cinematic-motion-language**. Adaptation points: the Seedance wrapper syntax ((...) music/<...> sound effects/{...} dialogue/【...】 subtitles) is adapted to the prompt capabilities of `generate_video_v3`; where no equivalent exists, convert to a sound-effect intent description and note it; the refer_type parameter (Kling 3.0 Omni non-member fallback), membership branch, and super-resolution (Jimeng SR/MediaKit) have no counterpart in this environment → legitimately deleted, see reconciliation; the "Midjourney banned for consistency" rule is converted to the "exploration images do not serve as consistency anchors" approach and retained; model proper names are adapted and rewritten to this environment's generation surface (not deleted).

## Workflow (5 milestone pauses; do not run everything in one go; use `ask_human` to present review checklists and obtain explicit confirmation before continuing)

1. **Final_Video_Spec** (`write_file_v2`): lock in global parameters—aspect ratio (default 16:9), target duration, output language (Japanese/Chinese lyrics), visual style tone (Heisei film idol MV), color system (summer youth film).
2. **Storyboard** (`storyboard`): first build the key_elements (member characters + core scenes + key props), then plan the shot list following the fixed structure **daily life → after school → friendship → practice → laughter → stage → youth climax → dusk wrap-up**, and sync the audio_layer (BGM + optional VO).
3. After the storyboard is complete, **scan existing user-uploaded materials** and bind matching resources to the corresponding Storyboard slots to avoid duplicate generation.
4. **Element reference images** (`generate_image_v3`): generate reference images for all key_elements (character reference images **two-panel: bust + full-body**; scene reference images marked with film tone).
5. **Per-shot videos** (`generate_video_v3`): prioritize element images as visual references.
6. **Audio** (`generate_audio`): BGM + sound-effect/VO audio layers.
7. **Compositing** (`composite_video`): timeline editing to export the final video.
> Dependencies: 2→1;3→2;4→2;5→4;6→2;7→4,5,6. **For the itemized checklists and question scripts of pause nodes A–E, see** `references/pause-nodes-and-vertical.md` (full text of the 5 nodes + vertical-exclusive pauses V-A/V-B + general scheduling for vertical short durations). User-uploaded materials are bound preferentially as the corresponding key_element assets; do not regenerate existing content.

## Short Video Version Structure (30s/60s/3min · prune by priority rather than compress proportionally, preserving narrative integrity; the full eight-part structure is used only for versions of 3min or longer)

- **30s (28–33s)**: Keep 3 parts—laughter climax (part 5, the energy peak, must not be deleted; compress to 2–3 shots, approx. 8–10s), stage performance (part 6, the idol core, 1–2 shots, approx. 6–8s), dusk ending (part 8, the emotional landing point, 1 shot, approx. 8–10s); delete daily life/after school/friendship/practice/youth climax. Rhythm: laughter climax shots compressed to 1.5–2.5s with a quick-cut opening; stage at 2–4s picked up directly with no transition; leave a full 8s for dusk as the only breathing space; BGM starts from the chorus burst point, no intro/verse. Total shots: 4–6.
- **60s (55–65s)**: Keep 5 parts—daily life (part 1, compressed to 2–3 shots, approx. 8–10s; prioritize scenery shots + 1 character close-up), friendship (part 3, 2–3 shots, approx. 8–10s; prioritize shot-reverse-shot cross-cutting), laughter climax (part 5, 3–4 shots), stage (part 6, 2–3 shots, approx. 10–12s), dusk ending (part 8, 1–2 shots, approx. 10–12s); deletion priority: practice (part 4) > after school (part 2) > youth climax (part 7); if still over, compress daily life to a single scenery shot (approx. 3s). Rhythm: daily life → friendship → laughter as three consecutive parts, progressing from slow (3–4s) → medium (2–3s) → fast (1.5–2.5s); BGM moves into the verse at the end of the intro and hits the chorus burst just before the laughter climax; the stage section counterpoints the chorus beats, and the dusk ending completes within the outro fade-out. Total shots: 10–14.
- **3min (2:50–3:15)**: Use the full eight-part structure; shot counts per part follow the standard templates as reference. Reference duration allocation per part: daily life 20–25s/5–7 shots, after school 15–20s/4–5, friendship 20–25s/5–6, practice 20–25s/4–5, laughter climax 15–20s/4–6, stage 25–35s/5–7, youth climax 20–25s/3–4, dusk ending 25–35s/3–4. Pacing compression priority (when over time): first compress practice (5→3 shots; drop the footstep close-up, keep the mirror wide shot and the expression medium shot), then compress after school (drop the school bag packing close-up; cut from the overexposed door push straight into the running follow shot), finally compress daily life (reduce to 2–3 purely scenery establishing shots); **do not compress** the laughter climax/youth climax/dusk ending (the emotional core). Total shots: 30–40.
- **Cross-version common rules**: ① Opening principle—the first shot of any version must establish a visual memory point within the first 3 seconds (backlit silhouette/smile close-up/iconic-scene scenery shot); opening with dialogue or expository visuals is prohibited. ② Ending principle—the last shot of any version must be of the dusk-ending type (frozen smile or sunset silhouette), no shorter than 5s, closing with a soft fade to warm white. ③ BGM counterpoint—annotate each part's corresponding BGM structural section (intro/verse/chorus/outro) at the storyboard stage, ensuring the laughter climax hits the chorus burst point and the dusk ending falls within the outro fade-out range. ④ **Non-separable shot pair**—the after-school run (part 2) and the overexposed rooftop door push (part 2) form a strongly continuous pair; when trimming, either keep both or delete both.
- **Reels short-duration BGM structure annotation specification** (15s/30s Reels; at the storyboard stage, annotate the corresponding BGM timestamp range and beat markers next to each shot): see `references/short-version-and-vertical-shot.md`.

## Vertical (9:16) Specifics (layered on top of the standard storyboard rules, not replacing them)

Key points: keep the subject centered in the vertical frame (approx. 15% safe margins top and bottom); the vertical frame is only 56% as wide as horizontal—prioritize medium shots, close-ups, and extreme close-ups; wide shots require the subject standing centered; no more than 2 people per frame is recommended—with 3 or more, stagger them vertically or split into multiple single-person close-up quick cuts; characters' eyes should fall in the upper 1/3 of the frame; scenery shots should prioritize compositions with strong depth (corridor perspective/riverbank extension/tram track vanishing point). **Subtitle safe zones**: top 8–10% (platform title bar; no important face/prop close-ups), bottom 20–25% (platform text/music/interaction buttons; no main expressions/key actions), right side 10–12% (vertical like/comment/share column; subject/key props must not touch the right edge), core expression zone at the central 70% (horizontal)×65% (vertical)—all emotional highs/key gestures/smile freeze frames must land in this zone.

## Element Images and Shot Video Generation (`generate_image_v3` / `generate_video_v3`)
**Character/Scene Concept Images** (`generate_image_v3`, with `ai_model` set to `LibNavo Pro`): recommended source is Nano Banana Pro (2K, strongest cross-image facial consistency, suitable for dual-panel character reference compositions and multi-character joint generation); during the style exploration phase, an exploration model may additionally be used to generate vibe references, but **exploration images do not serve as consistency anchors and are not used for character main images** (the source rule that Midjourney is banned for consistency is retained). **Shot Video Generation** (`generate_video_v3`):
- Default path MultiModalToVideo (source Seedance 2.0, 720p). Per-shot reference inputs: ① **element images (required)** — element images of all characters appearing in the shot + element images of scenes/props; ② **previous shot video (optional)** — add only when there is strong continuity with the previous shot (continuous action in the same scene / direct emotional continuation); usually skip the video reference to preserve the youth-montage sense of sharp cuts.
- Shots requiring synchronized sound effects/dialogue (e.g., stage performances) switch to FirstFrameToVideo (source Veo3.1 Fast, 720p), avoiding overlap conflicts with the standalone narration VO track (in this environment, mapped according to the first-frame/audio capabilities of `generate_video_v3`).
- **Vertical-specific generation path** (native 9:16 preferred: Wan2.6 when no sound effects are needed / Kling 3.0 Audio when synchronized dialogue and sound effects are needed; Seedance landscape generation → center-crop 56% fallback plan including composition preset terms/crop anchors/loss-assessment regeneration table; vertical for non-members): see `references/vertical-shot-generation.md` for the full text. Member branch/refer_type/super-resolution are adaptation points with no counterpart in this environment; see the reconciliation.

**Cross-Shot Character Facial Consistency Assurance**:
- **Consistency Checklist** (run each time a batch of shots is completed, i.e., every 3–5 shots or at the end of each section, before proceeding; if it fails, regenerate first):

| Check Item | Pass Criteria | Fail Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Facial recognition anchor | Face shape, eye shape, and hair color match the key_element reference image; visibly recognizable as the same person | Obvious drift in facial structure, sudden change in hairstyle/skin tone |
| Makeup and hairstyle | Bangs shape and hair-volume feel match the element description | Bangs type changed (soft bangs to center part), step change in hair color |
| Costume continuity | Same character's costume within the same section matches the element description | Costume style or color scheme changes within the section with no plot reason |
| Vibe baseline | Facial expression stays aligned with the vibe tags (energetic/shy/cool, etc.) | Character vibe clearly deviates from the established setting (e.g., energetic type turning cold/aloof) |

- **Regeneration criteria**: must regenerate — facial recognition anchor fails (face shape/eye shape clearly off-model), costume changes within the same section with no plot reason; regeneration recommended — vibe baseline deviates by more than 1 level, sudden hairstyle change affecting recognizability; acceptable to keep — momentary defocus from slight handheld movement, subtle natural skin-tone differences caused by lighting changes, natural expression fluctuation within a reasonable emotional range. For regeneration, prefer tuning the prompt (strengthening facial feature terms/changing the seed/reducing shot duration) over switching models directly; if the same prompt fails twice in a row, then consider switching to FirstFrameToVideo to strongly control first-frame appearance via keyframe.
- **5 strategies for reference-image binding in multi-member same-frame shots**: ① Bind an independent reference image for each appearing member (the left-panel half-body close-up is preferred as the facial anchor; bind sequentially with `{{Node1}}`/`{{Node2}}`; in the prompt, specify which member each placeholder corresponds to and its reference focus — face/full body/costume); ② member count cap — within a single shot, keep the same frame to no more than 3 members; beyond 3, faces are easily confused, so prefer splitting into multiple close-up/medium shots cutting between them; group wide shots may be relaxed to 5 members but with lowered expectations for individual facial accuracy; ③ protagonist first — when there is a clear center girl or an emotionally focal member, place their reference image in the first placeholder (the model gives more weight to the first image); ④ avoid reference-image conflicts — when hairstyles/costume color schemes are similar, distinguish with directional words ("the member on the left side of the frame references `{{Node1}}`"); if confusion persists, generate a single-member shot first and then composite multiple members; ⑤ group ensemble wide shots — when there are many members and facial recognition is not a primary requirement (long-shot running/stage wide shot), use a multi-character joint reference image (landscape multi-panel composition) in place of multiple independent reference images; only applicable to wide/long shots, while medium shots and closer still require separate binding.

## Compositing (`composite_video`)

**Editing temperament baseline**: "fragmented youth" — a montage like a Heisei idol MV, not a finely polished commercial edit.
- **Pacing & duration**: daily/friendship segments use fast-cut montage with single shots of 1.5–3s; emotional/dusk closing segments slow down to single shots of 4–8s with breathing room; stage performance cuts follow the BGM beat, with action peaks corresponding to cut points.
- **Transition preferences**: prefer soft dissolve (youthful nostalgia) / handheld smash cut (natural transition) / freeze frame on smile (freezing a smile); allow film timecode flash cuts / light VHS glitch transitions / DV camcorder-feel cross dissolves; **forbidden**: precision commercial VFX transitions / K-pop style RGB outline cuts / cyber visual glitch effects.
- **Audio layer mixing**: the main BGM track runs through the whole piece and fades out gradually in the dusk closing segment; ambient sounds (cicadas/trains/laughter) are layered beneath the BGM in daily/friendship segments at roughly 20–30% of the BGM volume; when narration VO is present, duck the BGM slightly during the VO (duck to about -6dB) and restore it after the VO ends; for video shots with built-in sound effects (generated by FirstFrameToVideo), take care that they do not overlap the VO track.
- **Visual treatment**: unify the overall color tone to a "summer youth film" palette (pastel warm tones, soft contrast) to avoid color jumps; keep slight handheld shake / occasional defocus (youth documentary texture, no stabilization); for the ending, a dusk freeze frame with a soft fade to warm white or a nostalgic film burn out is recommended.
- **Reels vertical BGM cutout points and beat alignment spec (15s/30s) + vertical fast-cut BPM beat quick reference table + vertical editing specifics (timeline 1080×1920/720×1280, no letterbox, subtitle safe zone, pacing tightened 20–25%, no horizontal slide transitions)**: see `references/assembler-reels-vertical.md` for the full text. Vertical audio mixing rules are identical to horizontal, with no vertical-specific adjustments.
