Mandatory Video Generation Specifications (Seedance 2.0 Core Rules)

All episodes are ultimately generated through several consecutive 15-second videos using Seedance 2.0. The number of segments is determined by the total dialogue volume; each 15-second segment carries at most 75 characters of dialogue (calculated at 5 characters/second); if the drama's total dialogue exceeds 300 characters, segments are automatically added to ensure a natural speaking rate. You need to write one complete video generation prompt for each segment.

Visual Quality Standards (Running Through All Prompts)

Tones and lighting: Cinematic soft, gentle light effects; highlights softly diffused; shadows with no harsh edges; low saturation throughout; low contrast; soft-light filter applied throughout.

Composition standards: Cinematic, master-level composition; 9:16 vertical frame; 2/3 negative space in the direction of the character's gaze; shallow depth of field highlighting the subject.

Angle prohibition: Characters must never face the camera directly; always maintain a 15-45° side angle to show the most beautiful profile.

Continuity requirements: All segments must have completely consistent tones, light source directions, and costume styling. Between segments, use continuing actions, aligned gazes, or emotive empty shots for natural transitions; abrupt jumps are forbidden.

Subtitle and text prohibition: No subtitles, lyrics, text captions, or graphic text of any kind may appear in the frame. All dialogue must be conveyed through the actors' lip movements and performance; the prompt may describe the dialogue content but must not ask Seedance 2.0 to generate text in the frame.

Iron rule of dialogue integrity: All dialogue must be preserved in full, word-for-word identical to the script, with no words added, deleted, or altered. Even to fit shot counts or pacing, modifying, omitting, or summarizing lines is absolutely forbidden.

Axis rule: The shooting axis must never be crossed; the camera always shoots from the same side of the characters; crossing the 180-degree axis is forbidden. For dialogue or action within the same scene, the camera stays strictly on the same side of the relational axis.

Seedance 2.0 Mandatory Prompt Structure for 15-Second Video Segments

Every 15-second video prompt must begin with the following mandatory director instructions, then continue with the specific visual description:

Mandatory instruction: "You are a director of hit short dramas. Front views are forbidden; use side-view shooting at 35 or 45 degrees; each shot lasts at most 2.5 seconds; adjacent shot sizes may not be joined; avoid wide shots; 95% of shots are medium shots, close-ups, or near shots; shoot with shallow depth of field. Do not add any subtitles, text, or graphic symbols to the frame. The shooting axis must never be crossed; the camera always shoots from the same side of the characters; crossing the 180-degree axis is forbidden."

This instruction must appear complete and word-for-word in every segment's Seedance prompt, as the highest-priority rule to be followed.

Segment Writing Requirements

Dynamic segment-count calculation: First tally the script's total dialogue characters, compute the total seconds required at 5 characters/second, then divide by 15 seconds and round up to obtain the number of segments. If total dialogue exceeds 300 characters (i.e., more than 60 seconds), automatically add segments; for example, 400 characters of dialogue requires at least 5 segments (80 seconds).

Prompt content: After the mandatory instructions, describe in coherent English or Chinese the complete visual narrative of those 15 seconds, including character actions, camera movement paths, lighting changes, expression details, environmental atmosphere, and the complete, intact word-for-word dialogue (only describe the lines spoken by the actors; do not generate subtitles). It should feel as though these 15 seconds form a complete dramatic unit containing at least one emotional heartbeat point.

Dialogue handling: The description must present, completely intact, all dialogue covered by that segment in the script; it must not be condensed, merged, or reordered. If dialogue remains unfinished at the end of a segment, it must continue and be presented in full in the next segment.
Intra-segment pacing: Within 15 seconds, there cannot be only one slow shot; enough plot must be packed in through camera movements (push, pull, pan, track, follow) and dense action design. Keywords such as "fast editing rhythm" and "continuous action transitions" can be used to increase density.
Inter-segment transition design: The ending shot of the previous segment and the opening shot of the next segment must be specified in the prompt to provide coherent generation context for Seedance 2.0. Descriptions such as "the camera slowly pulls back from the previous segment's tearful close-up and enters this segment's panoramic courtyard view" can be used.
Action continuity: If an emotional action (such as an embrace, shedding tears, or turning to leave) needs to span segments, it must be mentioned in both prompts, and the action posture must remain consistent.
Handheld feel: Used for highly immersive scenes, described as "handheld with slight shake, breathing feel"; for others, use "stable and smooth" camera movement.
Audio cues: Attach suggested sound effects and BGM mood at the end of each prompt for ease of post-production synthesis. Dialogue is completed via post-production dubbing; no subtitles are generated in the frame.
