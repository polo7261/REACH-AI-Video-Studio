# Dark Doll Fable Video Director｜Dark-Chamber Fairy-Tale Doll Full-Workflow Video Director

## Positioning

This Skill is responsible for one thing only: turning the user's dark fairy-tale doll-style idea into a complete video production plan that can feed into the 平台 / Codex / image-to-video workflow.

It is not a horror-film generator, and it does not just make doll posters. Its core is "exquisite, gothic, fairy tale, doll, lonely, grotesque, elegant, mildly eerie yet beautiful." All images, character assets, scene assets, prop assets, first frames, and last frames must serve video production.

Deliverable lengths it can cover:

- 5 seconds: a single micro-motion image-to-video clip, e.g. a blink, a raised head, a wind-up key turning softly.
- 15 seconds: one small suspense beat, e.g. a music box starting up by itself, a doll slowly lifting its eyes.
- 30 seconds: two escalating anomalies, suited to short ads or concept films.
- 60 seconds: four 15-second segments forming a complete dark fairy-tale short.
- 75 seconds: five segments, suited to assignments, competitions, and mood shorts.
- 90 seconds: six segments, suited to fuller fairy-tale fable shorts.

Out of scope:

- Gore, dismemberment, disgusting monsters, zombies, jump scares, and cheap ghost flicks.
- Modern urban horror, cyber horror, filthy ruins.
- Making characters scream, run, fight, or suddenly turn into monsters.
- Replicating specific film/TV characters, specific animated figures, or existing IP.
- Outputting only a style concept without landing on video storyboards and prompts.

## Input

The user can give just one sentence, or provide full production requirements.

Input fields:

- Theme: e.g. "a porcelain doll awakening in an old attic", "a rabbit doll in a moonlit forest", "the last performer of an old theater".

- Scenes: old toy theater, gothic castle, moonlit forest, dust-sealed attic, dollhouse, old toy cabinet.
- Props: wind-up key, music box, candelabra, pocket watch, rabbit doll, red curtain, glass eyeballs, old letters.
- Mood: lonely, quiet, forgotten, freshly awakened, gently enigmatic.
- Mild-horror level: default 2/5; gore level default 0/5.
- Duration and aspect ratio: 5s / 15s / 30s / 60s / 75s / 90s; default 9:16.
- Reference images: only material, lighting, atmosphere, color, and composition may be drawn from them; do not copy specific people or outfits.
- Deliverables: character images, scene images, first frame, last frame, storyboard, video prompts, editing plan, review-and-revision pass.

Defaults:

- Default goal: a complete video production package.
- Default aspect ratio: 9:16.
- Default duration: when the user doesn't say, give a 15-second structure + a 60-second extended structure + a 5-second validation shot.
- Default character: an exquisite porcelain doll girl or a clockwork doll.
- Default scene: pick one of three — old toy theater / gothic attic / moonlit forest.
- Default props: wind-up key, old music box, candelabra.
- Default motion: extremely restrained micro-movements.
- Default sounds: music box, wind-up mechanism, puppet joints, candle flame, wind, faint wooden-floorboard creaks.

## Internal Process & Knowhow

### 1. First determine whether this is a "video task"

If the user says "make a video / full workflow / storyboard / 60 seconds / 75 seconds / 平台 / Codex / image-to-video / shot-by-shot prompts / optimize skill", it must be handled as a video production package. Do not just deliver a character image, a poster, or a 5-second template.

If the user asks only for images, still define each image as one of "character asset / scene asset / first frame / last frame", so later video expansion is easy.

### 2. Positive criteria for the dark fairy-tale doll style

A good result should satisfy:

- Exquisite, not grubby.
- Mildly eerie but beautiful, without leaning on gore or jump scares.
- Clearly defined doll materials: porcelain skin, glass eyes, puppet joints, wind-up mechanism, lace, velvet.
- Restrained motion: like a sleeping doll being softly awakened.
- Props that tell stories: wind-up keys, music boxes, candelabra, shadows, and pocket watches can create anomalies.
- Scenes with a fairy-tale feel: old theaters, castles, attics, moonlit forests — not modern rooms.
- Elevated lighting: cold moonlight, warm candlelight, dust motes, shallow depth of field, layered shadows.
- Progression in the video: quiet space → prop anomaly → doll micro-movement → suspense ending.

### 3. Fairy-tale suspense formula

For every short film, write the "fairy-tale suspense formula" first, then the storyboard:

The forgotten space: the viewer first enters a quiet, exquisite space.\
The core doll: she/it sits still like an object.\
The trigger prop: a wind-up key, music box, candelabra, or pocket watch behaves abnormally.\
Micro-motion: the doll makes only one small movement.\
Uncertainty: a shadow, a gaze, or a sound hints that "it has awakened".\
Suspense ending: no explanation, no gore — just land on a beautiful yet unsettling image.

### 4. Duration structures

5 seconds: one micro-movement.\
First frame steady 1s → micro-movement 2s → prop/lighting response 1s → suspense freeze 1s.

15 seconds: one suspense beat.\
0-3s establish the fairy-tale space; 3-7s the doll shows a subtle anomaly; 7-11s a prop or shadow changes; 11-15s the doll looks toward the camera or freezes.

30 seconds: two escalating anomalies.\
0-8s space and doll; 8-15s the first micro-movement; 15-23s the prop anomaly expands; 23-30s suspense final frame.

60 seconds: four segments.\
0-15s establish the space; 15-30s the doll awakens; 30-45s the anomaly expands; 45-60s suspenseful close.

75 seconds: five segments.\
0-15s fairy-tale space; 15-30s the doll appears; 30-45s a prop starts by itself; 45-60s shadow/lighting anomaly; 60-75s the doll's micro-movement and an unknown ending.

90 seconds: six segments.\
Building on the 75-second structure, add a segment of "old-memory flashback or repeated stage action", while still keeping the slow rhythm and micro-movements.

### 5. Asset planning

Assets must be planned before the video. Asset IDs follow a unified scheme:

- DDF-CHAR-01: protagonist doll
- DDF-EXPR-01: expression / gaze asset
- DDF-STATE-01: character state asset
- DDF-SCENE-01: scene asset
- DDF-PROP-01: core prop
- DDF-LIGHT-01: lighting atmosphere
- DDF-FIRST-01: first frame
- DDF-LAST-01: last frame
- DDF-SHOT-01: shot keyframe

The asset table contains at least: asset ID, name, type, purpose, appearance description, material description, video-consistency anchors, image prompt, and negative prompt.

Character assets must spell out: face, eyes, joints, clothing, wind-up mechanism, posture.\
Scene assets must spell out: spatial layout, character position, main light source, prop placement.\
Prop assets must be able to drive the plot: a music box starting up, a wind-up key turning, a candle flame shifting, an anomalous shadow.\
The first frame must be quiet, with the action not yet happening.\
The last frame must carry a clear suspense hook, suited to a freeze frame and added captions.

### 6. How to write image prompts

Image prompts must serve video consistency.

They must include:

- Dark fairy tale, exquisite doll, gothic details.
- Porcelain skin, glass eyes, puppet joints, velvet, lace, wind-up mechanism, old toys.
- Moonlight, candlelight, dust motes, shallow depth of field, layered shadows.
- A clean, exquisite scene — not grimy, not gory.
- Composition suited to a video first frame, with a clear subject and low motion amplitude.

### 7. Storyboard table

The video storyboard must be output as a table with these fields:

Shot number｜Duration｜Segment function｜Visuals｜Character action｜Prop change｜Mood｜Lighting｜Camera movement｜Sound｜Assets used｜Video prompt

Storyboard rules:

- Each shot expresses only one core change.
- Few actions, strong emotion.
- No horror built from screaming, running, or jump scares.
- Camera movement prioritizes fixed positions, extremely slow push-ins, and slight lateral moves.
- Each segment ends with a pause frame that can be cut.
- The same character's face, hairstyle, clothing, and materials must not change.
- No unrelated characters are added.
- No sudden space changes.

### 9. Editing, Sound, and Color Grading

The editing should feel like a dark fairy-tale fable, not a horror mashup.

Recommendations:

- Hold shots longer; cut sparingly.
- Use candle-flame defocus transitions, dark-curtain wipes, wind-up mechanism close-up cuts, and moonlight sweep transitions.
- Music: music box, slow piano, glockenspiel, low-frequency strings, old theater ambience.
- Sound effects: faint clicks of puppet joints, winding mechanism, music box gears, candle flames, wind, wooden floors, distant bells, soft glass clinks.
- Color grading: deep blue, dark red, ivory white, antique gold, black velvet; contrast between cold moonlight and warm candlelight.
- Subtitles: like fairy-tale narration, short, without over-explaining.

Do not use screaming, loud pops, ghostly wails, flesh sounds, strong jump scares, or modern electronic horror sound effects.

### 10. Revision Logic

Minor fixes:

- Not eerie enough: add self-activating props, shifting shadows, abnormal candle flames—no gore.
- Doll-like quality insufficient: emphasize porcelain skin, glass eyes, puppet joints, wind-up mechanisms, and old toy textures.
- Atmosphere insufficient: strengthen moonlight, candlelight, dust, shallow depth of field, and layered shadows.
- Movement too large: change to a single micro-movement and extend the pauses.

Rerun when:

- The character's face changes, clothes change, or the character becomes a real person or a monster.
- Gore, disgusting monsters, jump scares, or running and screaming appear.
- The scene turns into a modern room or a cheap ghost film.
- Fingers/faces are severely distorted.
- The first-frame composition is completely broken.
- Prop or character relationships are lost and the suspense no longer reads.
