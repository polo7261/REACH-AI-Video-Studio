# Taiwan New Wave (Hou Hsiao-hsien × Edward Yang × Tsai Ming-liang) Film Pipeline

> 🔴 **Execute strictly according to this SKILL, step by step.** Version 2.5.7-rc1 (candidate; built on 2.5.6-rc1, ports the A24 screenplay-writing module with New Wave stylistic adaptations: voice mode added at the entry, action lines/performance tones added to the story step, and a new "Screenplay Writing · Narrative Rhythm & Dialogue" subsection). All tools are described as natural-language intents (image generation capability / video generation capability / video compositing capability / post-editing capability / ask user for confirmation / read-write files), mapped by the agent to actually available tools.

## Underlying DNA (unconditional, carried by every shot)
1. 🔴 **Long takes + fixed camera**: set the camera down somewhere and let things happen — fixed camera position as the norm, camera movement kept to a minimum; within a long take, storytelling relies on character blocking and depth-of-field staging, not on cutting or fast camera moves.
2. **Natural light + realism**: natural light as the primary source (window light / daylight / indoor practical light), no dramatic artificial lighting; authentic natural texture, everyday-life feel, not polished staged setups.
3. 🔴 **Medium-to-long shots + frame-within-frame composition + negative space**: medium-to-long shots as the norm, **close-ups kept to a minimum**; characters and environment are an inseparable whole; door frames / window bars / corridors / tunnels are brought into the frame to create layered framing and depth; attention to balance, negative space, and a slightly high-angle objective gaze.
4. **Low-saturation faded film texture**: colors lean gray and faded, low saturation, low contrast, shadows slightly lifted rather than crushed black (lifted blacks), medium-format-like ultra-fine film grain (nearly invisible) + soft highlight glow (halation/bloom) — **absolutely not vivid digital HDR**. ⚠️ **Faded ≠ dirty, old ≠ broken**: faded means clean, translucent low saturation with no gray haze; old means plain and aged yet respectable, lived-in by people (except in Tsai Ming-liang's damp, decaying direction), not peeling wall paint / mold / abandoned disrepair; shadows slightly lifted to preserve tonal layers, never crushed into a muddy dead-black mass; overall exposure leans bright, not darkened; for the cool-tone direction (Yang/Tsai), don't push it so cold it turns greenish — keep a trace of warmth underneath. Old yet clean, dark yet translucent, cool yet not frigid.
5. **Setting as narrative**: realistic environments, aged but tidy and respectable (signs of life but clean, currently inhabited, not run-down or filthy), carrying a sense of its era.
6. **Subtle environmental motion lets the frame breathe**: wind stirring curtains, shifting light and shadow, drifting smoke, flowing water, falling rain — subtle motion beyond the subject gives the static long take a sense of time.
7. 🔴 **Restrained, detached emotion (the soul)**: understated and suppressed, not acted out; emotion hides in the pace of movements / pauses / gazes / postures, conveyed through staging rather than facial close-ups.
8. 🔴 **Brisk, fast-leaning pacing; short individual shots; frequent cutting; yet every shot's content plays out in full**: the visual language (fixed camera / long takes / low saturation / frame-within-frame composition) stays faithful to the originals; brisk pacing suits short online videos, with short individual shots (one action 3–5 seconds) and no dragging — but an action / dialogue / entrance-and-exit / gaze must **occur completely within a single shot, given ample duration and never cut short**. Briskness comes from short shots / frequent cutting / crisp transitions, **never from cramming more content into one shot or chopping actions into fragments to speed up** (which would cause jumpy visuals and actions vanishing midway). The shot language remains fixed and restrained.

**Universal negatives (attached to every prompt)**: `anime, CGI, cartoon, 3D rendering, plastic-looking skin, over-stylization, dramatic artificial lighting, excessive close-ups, fast flashy camera moves, handheld shake, whip pans, fake HDR, oversaturation, garish vivid colors, crisp digital-video look, glossy modern sheen, theatrical acting style, frozen lifeless frame, peeling wall paint, mold spots, water stains, ruined and abandoned, shadows crushed into a muddy dead-black mass`

## Texture suffix (appended by default to every image prompt)
`shot on film, low-saturation faded color grade, low saturation and low contrast, shadows slightly lifted to preserve layers (lifted blacks, not crushed black), medium-format-like ultra-fine nearly invisible film grain, soft highlight halation glow, natural-light realism, fixed-camera long takes, observational medium-to-long framing with minimal close-ups, frame-within-frame composition with negative space, old yet clean not dirty, dark yet translucent, skin tones still real and alive with healthy color within the low saturation; no vivid digital HDR, no oversaturation, no peeling or moldy walls, no crushed dead shadows`

## Workflow (8 steps; 🔴 all user-facing questions must always be popped as option cards, with clickable options including a default — no open-ended questions)
1. **Unified entry questionnaire (pop an option card)**: one card asks all nine items; once answered, write to memory: ① Duration (single shot ≤15s / single episode 30–60s / multi-episode, several minutes / custom) ② Execution mode (fully automatic / manual) ③ Aspect ratio (**default 16:9**, options 4:3/16:9/21:9/custom; resolution defaults to 720p) ④ Director direction (general base / Hou / Yang / Tsai, default general base) ⑤ Emotional tone (calm restraint / light playfulness, default calm restraint; if playful, apply light-hearted adjustments such as the fifth deadpan-humor score) ⑥ 🔴 **Voice mode** (① pure atmosphere · no dialogue, no narration ② pure dialogue · dialogue only ③ pure narration · voiceover only, characters make no sound ④ dialogue + narration interleaved; **default ②**, stored as `VOICE_MODE`; this item determines whether the screenplay includes dialogue/narration — see step 3 and the "Screenplay Writing" subsection for details) ⑦ Dialogue/narration language (default Taiwanese-flavored Chinese, stored as `DIALOGUE_LANG`; if ⑥ selects pure atmosphere, this becomes "no language") ⑧ 3 core elements (living space / family relationships / everyday objects) ⑨ Story synopsis (yes/no: if yes, provide it to be followed; if no, generate from the 3 elements). Moodboard and story generation are deferred to steps 2 and 3.
2. **Style confirmation (generation action)**: generate a moodboard from the 3 elements (image generation capability, 4:3, a single fused image filling full bleed, no white borders / collages / panel splits) → store as the style anchor asset and record an archive marker.
3. **Story (generation action)**: if a synopsis exists, follow it and only layer on Taiwan New Wave stylization; if not, generate from the 3 elements (slice of life / era / family / restrained detachment / negative space). 🔴 Each scene must spell out concrete, filmable everyday action processes (with context), not emotional psychology. Output scene list + emotional arc + 🔴 **action lines** + 🎭 **character performance tones**, and **follow the voice mode selected at entry ⑥** (pure atmosphere: no lines or narration; pure dialogue: write dialogue; pure narration: write narration; interleaved: weave both; see the "Screenplay Writing" subsection below for details).
   - 🔴 **Action lines required**: each scene describes an everyday physical action process (start → middle → end), concrete behavior the camera can capture; every action must carry contextual motivation ("hearing the door and setting down the bowl to look up," not merely "looking up").
   - 🎭 **Performance tone required**: for every character with scenes, define a restrained, inward performance signature — signature micro-gestures / habitual postures (rubbing the bowl's rim when silent, shifting the gaze away when troubled, repeatedly smoothing a clothing hem while waiting) — consistent across shots throughout the film, for character action to draw on at the video stage. Emotional externalization relies on the pace of movements / pauses / gazes / postures, not on facial close-ups or theatrical acting.
   - 🔴 **Narrative pacing alignment**: the story's emotional arc must match the pacing paradigm in the "Screenplay Writing" subsection (default: calm, restrained slice of life; playful material uses deadpan humor), and propagate downstream into storyboard shot splitting.
   **After steps 2+3 complete, pop one merged confirmation card** (confirm moodboard + story together: all good, proceed / adjust moodboard / adjust story / adjust both) — step 4 only after approval, catching deviations early.
4. **Assets + storyboard sheet (pop an option card for unified confirmation)**: first produce assets — character model cards (face-locked), scene reference images (only one extreme-wide establishing shot per scene, anchored to the moodboard; all storyboard shots reference it, with angles framed via text), prop composite images; then, based on the story and assets, **compile the complete storyboard sheet** (per shot: shot size + @character action + camera move + environment + duration); the two are **bundled into one option card for unified confirmation (confirm as correct / needs adjustment; moodboard/story were already confirmed on the previous card and are not repeated), step 5 only after approval** (this gate is retained even in fully automatic mode).
5. **Generate video**: Generate item by item according to the confirmed shot list, attaching that scene's reference images/character images/prop images to lock consistency, 🔴 **do not attach the moodboard**. 🔴 Reference nodes in the prompt are always represented as **`{{node_key}}`** (double curly braces + the node's actual key name, e.g., `{{Mixed 1}}`), in the same order as the asset references — the only format the framework recognizes; use it from the very first submission, forbidden to use 〈〉/[ ]/self-invented formats (a wrong format will inevitably fail generation).
6. **Music**: After the video is finalized, **pop up an options tab to confirm the direction** (list the selectable branches + preselect defaults per director/tone), and generate the BGM (**mureka model by default**) — 🔴 once generated, **save it first and do not layer it in**, wait for step 8 to decide.
7. **Compositing**: Only split into multiple segments if it exceeds 15 seconds, then stitch them serially into the complete final film. 🔴 The first composite contains only video + ambient sound + dialogue/voiceover, with no BGM layered in.
8. **Unified processing & inquiry after the final film (pop up options tab)**: One box, multi-select: ① Add subtitles ② Composite background music ③ Change the music, then re-composite. Key constraints:
   - 🔴 **Write the subtitle option wording correctly per language**: Chinese → "Add Chinese subtitles"; **non-Chinese → must write "Add bilingual subtitles (original language + Chinese)", monolingual is forbidden**.
   - 🔴 **Execution order is forced: subtitles first, then music** (tool limitation: adding subtitles while music is present causes problems); if changing the music, regenerate first, then layer in this order.
   - 🔴 **Mixing defaults**: original audio +8dB, BGM -2dB (user-changeable).
   - Attached post-editing tools: subtitles (Chinese monolingual / non-Chinese bilingual), transitions (hard cut by default, added only if the user mentions it; options: fade-in/fade-out, black screen, white screen, grain dissolve), frame extraction, cropping, text overlays (era title card / location caption bar / name strip).

## Screenplay Writing · Narrative Pacing & Dialogue (🔴 ported from the A24 module with Taiwan New Wave stylistic adaptation)
- 🔴 **Default pacing = quiet restraint · slice of life · negative space**: no oppressive escalation, no strong plot hooks; emotions seep slowly out of the everyday, relying on the accumulation of time and negative space rather than twists. Tension comes from the constant undercurrent of "ambiguity of the situation," "estrangement in relationships," "something left unsaid" — not on gratification, not on suspense puzzle-solving. Open ending, no closure, no answers — life goes on, the emotions remain unresolved.
- **Emotion first, information restrained**: visuals and script serve inner emotion rather than delivering information; the audience often understands the situation one step later than the characters (creating restrained alienation); avoid genre-film-style strong hooks and expository scenes.
- 🎭 **Humorous subject (when entry ⑤ selects light & humorous)**: switch to **deadpan slice of life** — dead-serious absurdity, misaligned everyday life; humor comes from the contrast between situation and reaction, not from characters clowning or mugging; still keeps the New Wave base: restraint, negative space, open ending, refusal to preach (the score uses the fifth branch, the deadpan-humor sketch).
- ⚠️ **One film, one rhythm — no mixing**: at the start of each film set either "quiet restraint" or "deadpan humor," consistent across the whole film; local abrupt stops/contrasts are allowed, but the main tone is singular.
- **Pacing made actionable**: narrative pacing must align with the story step's emotional arc and cascade down into shot splitting — quiet restraint → sparse events, complete gaze within a single shot, frequent cuts but every shot plays out in full; deadpan humor → contrast created through the timing of misaligned situations and restrained reactions. Shots are still cut by content events (see the video iron rules and editing rhythm for details); pacing only affects event density and gaze duration, and does not change the principle of "cut by content, let every shot play out in full."
- 💬 **Dialogue (vocal modes that include dialogue, i.e., ②/④)**: 🔴 wherever two or more people share a scene, or a character has a motive to communicate, write actual spoken lines — don't just write actions and render the characters mute; the film must not run through with zero dialogue, leaving nothing but actions. Dialogue volume is set per scene (solitary/ultra-restrained scenes may have no lines, relying on silence and staging). Mark the speaker for every line. Dialogue text follows `DIALOGUE_LANG`, wrapped in {} (per the video iron rules). (No dialogue in pure-atmosphere ① and pure-voiceover ③ modes.)
- 🎭 **Dialogue style (Taiwan New Wave texture)**: everyday, colloquial, short — like what Taiwanese people would actually say day to day (natural Taiwanese flavor, minimal bookishness); 🔴 **keep subtext** — important emotions hidden beneath the surface of the words; characters often answer beside the point, say half a sentence, wrap real feelings inside everyday trivia (have you eaten yet, the weather, housework); **anti-aphorism**: no pretty lines that summarize the theme, no lines explaining one's own emotions (blunt declarations like "I'm sad" or "I'm lonely" are forbidden — use concrete everyday talk or silence instead); restrained, with pauses, with words held back, with silence. Avoid expository dialogue (spelling the plot out through mouths). Hou leans rural and warm-hearted, Yang leans urban, intellectual and cool, Tsai leans minimalist and near-wordless — fine-tune the register per the director's direction.
- 🎙️ **Voiceover (vocal modes that include voiceover, i.e., ③/④)**: restrained, intimate style — like writing to be heard by oneself, or talking to oneself; not omniscient exposition, not plot narration; never restates what the images have already expressed; a natural Taiwanese tone, minimal announcer voice. 🔴 **Voiceover text is finalized at the screenplay stage and written uniformly into the video prompt timeline** (per the New Wave video iron rules: write `off-screen voiceover{voiceover text}` immediately followed by "the voiceover is off-screen; on-screen characters do not speak and their lips do not move"; generated natively by seedance, no separate TTS/audio track, no post-hoc additions), equally bound by `DIALOGUE_LANG` and {} wrapping. ④ Interwoven mode: in the timeline, **assign dialogue and voiceover their own respective time slots**; voiceover only fills the inner/retrospective gaps dialogue cannot reach, never sounding at the same time as dialogue and upstaging it. (No voiceover in pure-atmosphere ① and pure-dialogue ② modes.)
- 🚫 **Do not write role sheets/character sheets**: no character sheet is produced by default, unless the user explicitly requests it.

## Character iron rules
- **Lock texture, loosen identity**: low-saturation faded film + medium-format fine grain + natural, non-plastic skin are hard constraints; hair color/hairstyle/clothing/era styling respect the user's settings, no Taiwan-New-Wave-style corrective overrides.
- 🔴🔴 **Ethnicity hard lock**: by default lock **East Asian/Taiwanese faces** (East Asian/Taiwanese); write it explicitly in both model sheets and shots, and exclude Western faces via negatives (without the lock, the model skews toward Western faces); ethnicity is independent of styling and does not drift with hairstyle or clothing. Exception: when the user explicitly specifies non-East Asian characters, follow the user.
- **Skin exception**: overall low-saturation fading, but skin tone keeps its real blooded color — never dragged into waxy yellow/ashen gray/lifeless pallor.
- **Model sheet ≠ final output**: pure white background + bright, even studio frontal lighting (face brightened, details first), never backlit/dim. Layout 16:9: left three views (front/side/back) + right large facial close-up (mandatory, cannot be omitted, must not be drawn as a full body).
- 🔴 **Character images carry no props**: the model sheet shows only the person themselves (figure/clothing/hairstyle/face), never carrying a backpack, holding an umbrella, book, Walkman, phone, or wearing non-essential accessories — props are managed uniformly via the prop composite image and referenced again in shots. Fitted clothing does not count as a prop.
- 🔴 **Model sheet template (fill in as instructed; changing the structure is forbidden, only replace the [] placeholders; if the layout falls apart, redo it)**:

## Scene image (🔴🔴 one scene, only one reference image · starting from v2.3.2, eliminating multi-image contradictions at the root)
> **One scene, only one reference image** (an extreme-wide/establishing empty shot, 4:3, no people), conveying the entire spatial layout / doors & windows / furnishings / depth. **All storyboard shots of that scene reference this same image**; angle/shot size are framed via the storyboard prompt text ("extreme wide" / "cut to a medium shot of X"), and **a second image for a different angle is absolutely never generated**. Reason: text-to-image renders different details on every regeneration (stove/cabinets/decor), so two images of the same scene will inevitably look like two kitchens; one image all the way = a single ground truth, leaving no room for contradiction. The Taiwan New Wave uses few camera setups, mostly the same angle at different shot sizes, so one wide shot suffices. (CAM multi-camera has been deprecated, because multi-image consistency couldn't be made reliable.)
- **Image generation**: one scene yields **only one image**, defaulting to an extreme wide/establishing shot (most complete information, convenient for framing each storyboard shot). 4:3 aspect ratio, empty shot with no people. If a storyboard shot needs a medium shot, write "push in from this space to X" in the prompt; do not switch images.
- 🔴🔴 **Moodboard attachment as a hard pre-check gate (must confirm before generation; no attachment, no submission)**: before producing this scene reference image, you must first confirm the moodboard style anchor is attached (the `{{moodboard node key}}` placeholder) — the sole source of unified color tone across the whole film; if it's omitted, tones fall out of sync. Strictly forbidden to submit without it attached; even in full-auto it must be explicitly included (in practice it's easily missed).
- **🎨 Attach moodboard**: this image uses the attached moodboard style anchor as reference (anchoring only color tone / light feel / texture, not composition, to unify the film's color tone).
- **🔴 No split-screen**: this is one complete picture, single-viewpoint, continuous composition; absolutely no multi-panel split-screen collage (a multi-panel composite fed into seedance gets misread as multiple reflections polluting the frame). The opposite of the character model card's multi-views-in-one.
- **memory record**: a one-sentence floor plan (orientations of doors/windows/furnishings) + light source direction + the scene's unique reference image node. All storyboard shots reference this one image.
- **Angles via text, not via swapping images**: for different storyboard shots of the same scene, angle/shot size rely entirely on the storyboard prompt's textual description (extreme wide → push in to medium → oblique view past the door frame); the reference is always that single unique image. Character position and orientation are anchored to **fixed in-scene reference objects** ("mother sits on the window side, facing the door"; 🔴 never use "left/right of the frame" for character position — frame left/right flips with the camera and would cause seat swaps). Extreme reverse angles: seedance improvises on its own, but since there is only one ground truth, the hard contradiction of two kitchens cannot arise; a scene that genuinely needs multiple radically different angles is an exception and requires manual confirmation.
- Main prompt template (default extreme wide/establishing shot): `one continuous, complete framing of the same scene, a single-viewpoint photographic image. [Scene name] extreme wide establishing shot: [relative positions of doors/windows/furnishings], [light source direction], [depth relationships], [realistic scene description — aged but tidy and respectable, with period feel and traces of daily life yet clean, absolutely not dilapidated or in disrepair], [director-card light and color], framed composition through door frames / window bars where appropriate, layered depth of field (deep focus), naturalistic natural light, low-saturation faded film, low contrast, shadows very slightly lifted to preserve layering (lifted blacks, not crushed black), medium-format-grade ultra-fine almost invisible film grain, clean and airy, old but not dirty, no people whatsoever, completely empty, pure environmental empty shot, one photographic image, a single viewpoint, no split-screen, no panels`
  Negative (standalone box): `split-screen, multiple panels, multiple viewpoints, grid, collage, reference image board, peeling wall plaster, flaking paint, mildew spots, water stains, cracks, ruined and abandoned, filthy and messy, shadows crushed into murky black mush, peeling paint, mold, water damage, crumbling wall, derelict, any people, high saturation, vivid colors, fake HDR, CG rendering, dramatic artificial lighting, white borders, glossy modern look`

- **Hou Hsiao-hsien · rustic gaze**: warm fading (earthy yellow/tea brown/warm gray) + nostalgic rustic cramped interiors, order amid clutter + train/tunnel/rail depth + framed composition + deep focus layering. Very few close-ups; fixed camera, objective gaze.
  - 🚂 **Signature · railroad-tunnel depth gaze (must appear at a high standard)**: strong natural light pouring in with depth from the tunnel mouth / the far end of the rails; train/rails/tunnel as framed composition and depth-leading lines; figures small in medium-to-far shots, embedded in the rustic environment; extremely deep depth of field with foreground/midground/background clearly separated; warm faded tones with halation; no close-ups. - **Edward Yang · urban geometry**: slightly cool neutral gray (blue-gray/concrete gray/cool white, **don't over-cool into bluish; keep a hint of warm base; overall exposure on the bright side, not darkened**) + urban glass curtain walls / window-frame geometric division + layered glass reflections + modern alienation. Fixed camera at medium-to-far range, very few emotional close-ups.
  - 🏙️ **Signature · Yi Yi glass-reflection layering (must appear at a high standard)**: shot through a glass curtain wall, city neon/street scenes/sky reflections layered with the face/interior behind the glass into one plane (inner and outer layers both sharp; not a single layer, not a transparent window); geometric division by window frames and architectural lines; figures small, enveloped by the structure; cool tones (blue-gray/cool white, restrained neon used only as an accent); no close-ups. ## Character action iron rules (no stiff standing, but everyday-life natural)
Every storyboard shot is bound to one specific Taiwan New Wave–style slice-of-life action; rigid standing / posing for the camera is forbidden. In multi-person shots everyone must be moving, each person with at least one micro-movement (unless the user requests stillness). Four-piece set = shot size (framed from the scene reference image) + concrete everyday-life action (with context) + camera movement + environmental dynamics. Action vocabulary: eating, serving rice, clearing bowls; washing dishes, doing housework; smoking, lighting a cigarette, gazing out the window; walking or cycling through streets, alleys, field ridges; waiting, sitting idle, zoning out; writing letters, reading letters, listening to the radio; lingering by doorframes and windows, peering out; moving slowly at the edge of rain puddles; family sitting around, each in silence. Break actions down into filmable body-part micro-movements (hands / head-neck orientation / torso / gait). Externalizing emotion: understated and inward, not performed — conveyed through pace of movement / pauses / gaze / posture; mostly medium-long shots, very few close-ups.

## Camera Movement (one type per shot; default fixed and restrained)

Fixed-position long take (default mainstay) / framed-composition gaze (signature) / depth-of-field zoom blocking / extremely slow pan or follow pan / wide empty shots with negative space / ⚙️slow push-in (rare, only at emotional key points). 🚫 No flashy rapid camera moves, handheld shake, whip pans, jump cuts or fast cutting, or abrupt push-pull changes.

## Editing Rhythm (brisk but not chopped up)

Keep fixed camera positions / blocking within long takes / medium-long framed gaze / restrained negative space; the rhythm is **brisk-to-fast, short single shots, frequent cuts, suited to web short videos** (noticeably brisker than the originals, no multi-minute ultra-slow long takes), but **each shot's content segment must play out in full** — one action / dialogue / entrance-exit / gaze happens completely within a single shot with ample duration, never split across several camera positions in progression. Briskness comes from short single shots / frequent cuts / clean transitions, never from cramming more content into one take and chopping up actions (chopping causes seedance frame jumps and actions vanishing midway). 🔴🔴 **Shot size jumps two steps by default** (e.g., extreme wide shot → medium shot / wide shot → medium close-up) — a one-step jump changes the picture too little and the cut still reads as a jump cut; only two steps is distinct enough to feel like a genuinely new shot; **no violent jumps** (three or more steps, e.g., extreme wide shot ↔ close shot); **when static at the same position/angle, jump at least one step** (same-angle cuts are differentiated by a one-step shot-size change, never a zero-step hard cut in place); if neither shot size nor angle changes, shoot it as one continuous long take and don't cut. Medium-long shots predominate. From v2.3.2 on, with one shared scene reference image, be especially careful not to write two shots that differ by only a tiny bit and read as a jump cut. Explicit hard cuts between shots; continuity carried by action and staging; no reliance on post-production transitions.

## Music (four tracks, aligned with the directors' actual film scores; BGM purely instrumental, generated only after the video characters are finalized; 🔴 store it after generation, confirm via the unified prompt box after compositing, subtitles first then layer the music before it enters the final cut)

- Temperament (shared by all four): restrained, spacious, detached, never melodramatic; music and picture each go their own way, no pushing along with the plot, no leitmotif reinforcement, generous breathing silences, relying on timbre and atmosphere rather than beat.
- 🎻 **Track 1 · Rustic Folk (Hou Hsiao-hsien's early rustic period)**: Chen Ming-chang-style rustic folk — single-line, clean simple melodies on yueqin (moon lute) / acoustic guitar / di-xiao flutes / erhu, derived from Taiwanese opera's unmetered phrases; plain and gentle, rustic nostalgic warmth, light and breezy (Dust in the Wind / The Puppetmaster / A Time to Live, A Time to Die).
- 🎛️ **Track 2 · Contemporary Electronic Fusion (Hou Hsiao-hsien's mid-to-late period)**: Lim Giong-style abstract fusion of electronica and Eastern folk — electronica as the base, melted together with Eastern folk poetics and ethnic timbres (xun / xiao / di / guqin / pipa); experimental, urban present-tense feel, detached and unbridled (Goodbye South, Goodbye / Millennium Mambo / The Assassin). ⚠️ Strictly distinct from Track 1; never mixed.
- 🎹 **Track 3 · Cool Cerebral Piano (Edward Yang)**: Kaili Peng / *Yi Yi*-style minimalist solo piano — sparse, restrained, limpid, cerebral, spacious; single notes leaving generous room, calm observation without melodrama, the detachment of urban modernity. ⚠️ Edward Yang is not electronic ambient; it is cool minimalist piano.

## Video Iron Rules
- 🔴 **Multiple shots per clip (mandatory by default)**: seedance max 15 seconds per generation, natively supports multiple shots within one clip + inter-shot transitions; do not use "one shot per clip + post-production splicing". 🔴🔴 **Duration-content budget check (do this first before writing shots, prevents overstuffing and chopping)**: First set the total duration (≤15s), then work backwards from "how many seconds each complete segment needs to play out" to determine how many segments this clip can hold, rather than fixing N shots first and forcing content in. Rule-of-thumb anchor: one complete everyday action (pushing the door open and entering / sitting down and lighting a cigarette / holding a bowl and eating / handing over or receiving an object) usually takes **3~5 seconds** to play out; a single clip usually has **3~5 shots** (3 shots×5s / 4 shots×3~4s / 5 shots×3s); each shot is still one complete action played out, not chopped up — cut frequently but don't chop actions up. If content overflows, cut segments without shortening individual shot durations, and start a new clip for the remainder. Single-clip duration is any integer from 4 to 15 seconds; only when exceeding 15 seconds do you split into multiple clips spliced serially. 🔴 Complete actions with clear start and end (entering/exiting frame / opening-closing doors / standing up / handing-receiving objects) still get a full 3~5 seconds to occupy a dedicated shot and play out in full; don't cram them into a clip where they get cut off (fixes: customer walks in, shadow flickers once and vanishes; jumpy shots). The prompt advances second by second; each shot = shot size (citing the scene reference image at the corresponding angle) + @character action + camera movement + environment, with explicit hard cuts between shots. Multiple shots within one clip share the same scene: 🔴🔴 Character positions are **always anchored to fixed reference objects** (window / door / deity altar / cabinet), e.g. "Mom by the window side, son with back to the door, Dad by the deity altar side", and **this set of fixed relationships is restated in every shot**; **across shots, "frame left/right" is forbidden for describing character seating** (frame left/right flips with camera position, and seedance will rearrange seating literally per the new shot's left/right — the culprit behind characters swapping seats); all shots within one clip default to being **shot from the same side** (write one line in [Overall scene and atmosphere]: "all shots in this clip are shot from the X side toward the Y side"), never circling to the opposite side. 🔴🔴 **The first shot (shot 1) must have main characters present with action (even a tiny back view in a long shot), never written as a pure empty establishing shot** — [Overall scene and atmosphere] already pins the space; if the first shot's timeline only describes space without people, seedance will produce an empty scene with no one; empty shots are reserved only for between-shot transitions or breathing room in the final shot. Every shot by default contains at least one @character + action; empty shots are the exception and require annotated intent. 🔴🔴 **Mention all present characters (fixes dropped characters / strangerization / disappearance)**: every character present in a shot's frame (including background / secondary / no-dialogue ones) must be called out in that shot's prompt with `@CharacterName` + position and action written (positions via reference objects: e.g. "@Dad reading the paper by the deity altar side, @Mom wiping her hands at the kitchen doorway", no "frame left/right"); anyone omitted will be generated by seedance as a stranger face or vanish; every character present in this shot gets their character card attached to the references (when hitting the ≤7 limit, choose by importance). 🔴🔴 **Group scenes: everyone in motion**: in multi-person shots every present person must have an action — the protagonist has the main action, everyone else at least one lifelike micro-action each (picking up food / wiping hands / flipping a newspaper / nodding / waving a fan), **no one may freeze like a statue** (anyone whose action is omitted will stand stiff and vacant); sole exception: when the user specifically requests stillness, follow the user and explicitly write "deliberately still".
- **Spatial continuity**: maintain consistency by restating the same set of spatial facts in prompt text (layout / light source / characters' relative positions and orientations), citing the corresponding scene reference image when changing angles.
- **No BGM**: video generation only wants ambient sound + dialogue; BGM is layered on at the compositing stage. Prompt permanently includes "keep only ambient sound and dialogue, no background music"; negatives add "background music, soundtrack".
- 🔴 **Language lock**: every video prompt permanently declares "dialogue/narration always in [DIALOGUE_LANG]; even model-improvised lines use only this language; no lines/subtitles/lip movements in other languages" (write it whether or not there is dialogue), dialogue wrapped in {}; negatives add "lines in other languages, mixed-language lip movements".
- 🔴 **Narration goes into the video prompt uniformly**: no separate TTS / separate audio track — narration text is written directly into the timeline and generated natively by seedance, written as `Off-screen narration{narration text}` followed immediately by "**the narration is an off-screen voiceover; on-screen characters do not speak and their lips do not move**" (a strong constraint preventing it from being pinned onto a character's mouth); it is equally subject to the language lock and {} wrapping, with a natural tone carrying Taiwanese flavor and minimal announcer cadence.
- **No dissolves or cross-fades**: hard cuts between shots; negatives permanently include no dissolve/cross-fade/fade.

## Post-generation self-check (redo if below standard)
Are the shadows crushed to dead black, muddy, or dirty (forbidden; must be slightly lifted to preserve layering)? Are they vivid digital HDR (forbidden; must be low-saturation faded)? Is the lighting dramatized and artificial (forbidden; must be natural light)? Are there lots of close-ups (forbidden; must be predominantly medium and long shots)? Are fixed-camera long takes / frame-within-frame compositions present? Is it aged but not dirty, with no excessive peeling wall paint / mold stains / dilapidation (except for the Tsai Ming-liang direction)? Do the characters have East Asian faces (if non-East Asian was not specified, mistakenly generating Western faces means redo)? Do characters mistakenly carry props (if carried, redo)? Is the space continuous across shots (character positions anchored with reference objects and restated in every shot, no "frame left/right" descriptions of seating, the entire strip shot from the same side)? Do multiple people swap seats (if swapped, redo)? Do adjacent shots default to a two-step jump in shot size with a change distinct enough (a single-step jump easily reads as a jump cut), is there any violent jump of three or more steps (forbidden), any zero-step hard cut in place from the same camera position (forbidden; same-angle cuts must jump at least one step or be changed to a continuous long take)? Is a character present in the first shot (forbidden to output a pure empty scene with no one)? Are all characters present in each shot (including background/secondary) mentioned in the prompt via @character name and attached with model cards (omitting anyone turns them into passersby / makes them disappear)? Do all characters in multi-person scenes have actions, with none frozen like statues (unless the user requested stillness)? Does the content volume match the duration (one action takes 3~5 seconds, a single strip usually has 3~5 shots; if too much is crammed in so each shot can't finish its action, trim segments / start a new strip)? Are the actions lifelike, with process and context included, emotions restrained and hidden in posture? Signature shots: Hou—depth leading lines / warm fading / tiny figures; Yang—layered reflections with both inner and outer layers in focus / geometry / cool tones; Tsai—a lone figure swallowed / water imagery / gloomy dampness / extreme stillness. If not, rewrite.
