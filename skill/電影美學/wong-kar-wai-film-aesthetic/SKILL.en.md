## One Set of Wong-Style Base Texture + One Optional Dominant-Color Line (Distilled in Stage 1, Unified Across the Entire Film)

Wong-style has only **one set of rendering base texture** (frame-within-frame imbalance, telephoto shallow depth of field, tungsten neon, step-printed motion trails, 35mm grain, live-action photorealism), locked down by the scene seed to guarantee consistency across the entire film. The palette has **no fixed preset** (the style is already embedded in the seed); instead, **a one-line dominant-color tendency is distilled from the script's emotion** and appended to the MJ prompt; if the user doesn't specify, the seed's native palette has the final say.

- **Base-texture clause (for MJ scene images)**: `Richly saturated cinematic imagery, frame-within-frame composition shot through foreground doorframes/window panes/curtains/glass, off-balance off-center composition with generous negative space, telephoto compression or slight wide-angle distortion, shallow depth of field, live-action photographic realism, warm-toned tungsten lamps and neon light, richly saturated tones, fine 35mm film grain, pervasive haze and smoke, slight vignetting, a nostalgic, melancholic, dreamy atmosphere`
- **Leading style sentence (placed as the first sentence of the Seedance prompt)**: `Richly saturated cinematic imagery, frame-within-frame composition shot through foreground elements, off-balance off-center composition, telephoto compression, shallow depth of field, live-action photographic realism, warm-toned tungsten lamps and neon light, richly saturated tones, fine 35mm film grain, pervasive haze, step-printed slow motion with motion trails, natural slight handheld sway with a breathing feel, subtle lens drift rather than an absolutely locked-off shot, a nostalgic, melancholic, dreamy atmosphere.`

**Optional one-line dominant-color tendency (distilled from the script's emotion, appended after the base-texture clause, not mandatory)**:

- Ambiguous warm red: `Warm-red-leaning palette — deep red, amber, gold, warm-toned shadows, accented by a single spot of neon`
- Cool neon tones: `Neon-leaning palette — cyan, azure blue, magenta, neon glow rising off a black rainy-night base`
- Jade green with warm gold: `Green-leaning palette — jade green, emerald, warm golden lighting, dark wood tones`
- Nostalgic faded warmth: `Faded-nostalgic palette — warm brown, aged ochre, grey-cyan, warm grain`
- Or write another line based on the script; at most one slightly brighter neon accent color, with everything else pressed into richly saturated warm tones.

## Fixed Seed & Image-Generation Specs (Quick Reference)

- **Scene/tone images = Midjourney V8.1** (**default quality "Adaptive"**), fixed seed `--sref 7054326907 --profile raz4xnf`, **wide shots with no people, zero text, frame-within-frame imbalance, live-action photographic realism** (characters are composited into the frame later in Seedance).\
  Tail string: `… --sref 7054326907 --profile raz4xnf --ar <16:9|4:3|1:1|9:16> --v 8.1 --no people, text, words, letters, numbers, subtitles, signage, logos, watermarks, cartoon, illustration, miniature model, flat symmetrical composition, pastel, bright even flat lighting, cigarettes, smoking, swirling smoke, lighters`

  > **Hard cap on MJ prompt length: total character count of the entire prompt (description sentence + tail string + negative words) ≤ 1024**. If exceeded, **only trim the description sentence at the front** (merge near-synonyms, delete secondary details), **never delete or alter the seed /** `--profile` **/** `--ar` **/** `--v` **/ key negative words**.

- **Characters + three-view styling sheets = Lib Image** (**Mandatory: all character/styling images must be generated with Lib Image; never use MJ or any other tool to produce people; MJ only produces people-free scene images**; **default "Standard quality"**): **generate all characters together in a single pass** — one **16:9 landscape image per character** (containing **4 front-facing full-body looks**; **the background must be a clean pure-white base and the figures must be full-body**; **the 4 sets must be 4 clearly different styling proposals — wardrobe (the whole outfit's style / silhouette / individual-garment pairings must all change, never the same outfit with only the color or pattern swapped), hairstyle, hair color, makeup, and accessory details** must all be **clearly differentiated from one another**, so the user can pick among 4 genuinely different directions; all 4 sets stay within the 90s–00s Hong Kong style and are all alternate looks for the same character). Once all characters are generated, **do the pick-one-of-four / confirmation for each in a single pass**; after confirmation, **use each character's selected look image as the image input (edit/img2img), use it directly as the middle (front) view, and only generate the missing left/right views**, producing all characters' white-background three-view sheets together, **real human models**; **never regenerate the three views from a rewritten text description** — face and wardrobe details (hairstyle and hair color, style and cut, collar and cuffs, buttons and pockets, prints and patterns, fabric, shoes and socks, accessories, every single color) are copied item by item from the selected image; only the viewing angle changes.

  - **Four-choice look image rendering preset (oiliness fix + cinematic feel, used only for the four-choice look-dev, must always be stacked)**: when generating the 4 front-facing looks, besides the wardrobe spec, these three groups of words must be layered on —

    - Skin (anti-oiliness): `matte realistic skin, visible pores and natural texture, no retouching, no plastic sheen, no oily highlights, no beauty filters, subtle film grain on the skin`
    - Tone (cinematic feel): `cinematic film-still quality, shot on 35mm motion picture film, anamorphic widescreen lens characteristics, slight halation in the highlights, filmic contrast, low-saturation period-style color grading, soft directional lighting`
    - Casting (anti-idol face): `a real film actor's face — full of character and lived-in feel, not a contemporary idol/influencer/Korean-style cosmetically sculpted face; natural eyebrow shape, no glass-skin dewy filter, no heavy modern makeup, no commercial fake smile, a restrained melancholic temperament`

    > **Background & framing**: the four-choice look images are **clean pure-white-background, full-body** studio look shots (`pure white seamless background, full body in frame`); the "cinematic film grading" above applies only to **skin texture / lens characteristics / color grading**, **adding no environment or scene into the image** — the white background stays unchanged.\
    > The three-view sheets carry over via i2i, **inheriting** skin and face from the selected image, **without layering the cinematic film grading again** (keep the white background photographic; lock only the geometry); the cinematic quality is settled once at the four-choice stage and naturally carries down to the three views and Seedance.

  - **Three-view sheet layout (hard constraint, must be followed)**: **everything must be completed within a single image** (no splitting across multiple images). **Generate only the full-body left / middle / right three views** (middle = front = the selected look image; left = full-body left side; right = full-body right side), three full bodies side by side; **no big heads / head close-ups**. Tidy layout, figures aligned at equal height, white background free of clutter.

  - **With source-face material** → the three-view **covers the face completely with a pure white circular sticker + small Chinese text in the bottom-right corner: 「人脸单独提供 · 此图仅用于服装造型参考」 ("Face provided separately · This image is for costume styling reference only")** (identity is locked by the source face in Seedance; the three-view is a behind-the-scenes tool image that never enters the film, so this label text does not violate the zero-text red line); **without a source face** → the three-view keeps the face (the face is the identity).

  - Key point (an edit instruction using the selected look image as the image input, not a from-scratch description): `Use the selected reference image as the image input, keep the exact same person and wardrobe, use it as-is for the middle (front) view, and only additionally generate the same model's left-side and right-side full-body views; output a single image containing one row of three full-body shots (left/middle/right), no head close-ups or big heads; figures aligned at equal height; replicate every detail from the reference image 1:1 — the same face, hairstyle and hair color, garment tailoring and silhouette, collar/cuffs/hem, buttons/zippers/pockets, prints and patterns, fabric texture, shoes, socks, every accessory, every single color; do not repaint the face; do not redesign, add, or remove any wardrobe detail; pure white seamless background, even soft studio lighting, photographic realism (**only when a source face exists: cover the face area of every view completely with a pure white opaque circular sticker, and add one line of small Chinese text in the bottom-right corner of the whole image: 「人脸单独提供 · 此图仅用于服装造型参考」 ("Face provided separately · This image is for costume styling reference only")**; when there is no source face, keep the face as normal)`

  - Generate multiple characters together in a single pass and maintain a **cast sheet** (name / identity / wardrobe / the character's color within the palette). Note: Lib Image has no `--sref`; consistency relies on detailed character cards + reference images + "the same model across views".
- **Video = Seedance 2.0**, input = **scene images of all locations involved in that storyboard segment** (if one 15s segment spans multiple locations, feed multiple scene images; if it takes place in a single location, one is enough) + **the same single three-view sheet** for each character (**used as both the portrait reference and the costume/styling reference**, locking both face and styling/body shape; **no longer feed the pick-one-of-four styling selection image**) + (when an original face exists) the original face image/video as the **identity anchor**; for multi-character shots, feed each character's own three-view sheet.

## Workflow (pause for confirmation at each stage)

### Stage 0 · Pre-Shoot Inquiry Card (ask everything in one continuous card and record it all in a single batch; **do not stop after each question**)

**Merge the 7 confirmation dimensions below into a single inquiry card, fire them at the user consecutively, and record everything uniformly once all answers are collected in one pass** (no pausing item by item):

1. **Script / Text** (main driver): Do you have a complete script/text? Or just a one-line idea (which I'll draft from)? Or nothing at all (let's talk out a concept together)?
2. **Characters + Styling**: ① Any face/character reference (image or &lt;15s video)? ② Any styling direction/styling reference images? (**Styling defaults to the 90–00s Hong Kong look; don't ask about the era**)
3. **Spoken language**: what language for the inner monologue/dialogue? (Defaults to the script's own language; for Cantonese, see the Stage 6 subtitle special case)
4. **Subtitles**: do you want subtitles? If yes, **choose the language: Simplified Chinese / Traditional Chinese / English / Bilingual** (confirmed once here; Stage 6 adds them per this choice without asking again; **when the spoken lines are Cantonese, subtitles render the Mandarin written version in this chosen Simplified/Traditional**, see Stage 6).
5. **Resolution**: pick one of **480p / 720p / 1080p**.
6. **Aspect ratio**: pick one of **16:9 (recommended) / 4:3 / 1:1 / 9:16** (**the user must choose; never silently default to 16:9**).
7. **Duration**: pick one of **15s / 30s / 45s / 60s** (this determines segment count: 15→1 segment, 30→2 segments, 45→3 segments, 60→4 segments; each segment = one 15s Seedance clip).

> Collect all 7 items in one pass and record them into Final_Video_Spec + cast notes. Only **after that** branch according to the script situation: full script → go to Stage 1; one-line idea/concept given → **draft a short script from it** (with characters, scenes, key narrative beats, a few lines of inner monologue and sparse dialogue, fitted to the chosen duration, mood veering hazy/melancholic; may weave in classic Wong Kar-wai props as hooks, see below) → submit to the user for confirmation/revision → once confirmed, go to Stage 1. Characters branch into the three Cases below.\
****Prop hooks for story ideas (usable when drafting the script / when given an idea)**: weave in props in the manner of Wong Kar-wai's classic films as hooks — e.g., **an expired pineapple can**, **watch / clock / countdown**, **phone booth / pager**, **convenience store / fast-food joint**, **rain and raincoats**, **dates / numbers (like "1966" "2046")**, **qipao**, **sunglasses**, **old boat ticket / can label**, etc.; **note: no smoking in frame, no cigarette props**.

**Three Cases (in every case, first "understand the styling direction + expand it into a complete written spec combining Wong-style Hong Kong fashion language", then generate — not just feed the image in and copy it; afterwards, uniformly: Lib Image generates the front-facing full-body look → confirm → Lib Image generates the white-background three-view sheet)**:

- **Case 1: styling reference image exists**: the styling image serves as the image reference + write the supplementary spec; Lib Image generates the look/three-view; if there's no face, the three-view keeps the face (face = identity).
- **Case 2: face exists, no styling image**: the face image serves as the image reference + write the supplementary spec; Lib Image generates the look/three-view; **bring the original face image into Seedance to lock the face; on the three-view sheet, cover the face with a white round sticker + small Chinese text in the bottom-right corner: "Face provided separately · This image is for costume/styling reference only"**.
- **Case 3: neither exists**: ask follow-ups for **gender (male/female) + ethnicity (Asian/Western/other)** (the era is already locked to the 90–00s Hong Kong look), write the supplementary spec, and Lib Image directly generates the look/three-view; **the three-view keeps the face (face = identity), and that three-view sheet serves as Seedance's portrait + costume/styling reference**.

**Wong Kar-wai-style Hong Kong fashion language (align with this when writing the supplementary spec; default locks to the 1990–2000s Hong Kong urban look, split by gender, and male looks never reuse female ones)**:

- **Female**: `slim-fit satin slip dress / padded-shoulder blazer / high-waisted straight-leg jeans / leather jacket / knit vest / plaid / silk shirt; choker, small tinted sunglasses, gold hoop earrings, hair clips; voluminous big waves or center-parted straight hair, thin high-arched 90s brows, matte true-red or brown-rose lips, minimalist modern contouring`
- **Male**: `Hong Kong male-lead aura — oversized tailored suits / leather jackets / open shirt over a white ribbed tank top / pleated trousers, center-parted curtain hair or slicked-back hair, tinted sunglasses, vintage watch; a lean, composed silhouette` (**no smoking in frame, no cigarettes**)
- **Shared**: `authentic silk/leather/denim textures with slight signs of wear; cuts and proportions true to the era; no contemporary minimalism, no athleisure, no brand-new costume-shop feel; colors stay rich and saturated within the film's palette`

The mood profile doesn't change the styling baseline; it only fine-tunes the color scheme along with the script.

### Stage 1 · Script Analysis + Final_Video_Spec

**Script analysis (narrative backbone; mandatory output, requires confirmation)**:

- Narrative structure: whether chapter-based / **inner monologue (first-person VO) arc** / narrative beats (15s takes 1–2 beats, 30s takes 2–3 beats, 45s takes 3–4 beats, 60s takes 4–5 beats).
- **Character list**: list all characters who appear + each one's identity/relationships.
- **Scene list**: the space of each scene.
- **Inner monologue, line by line**: mark the speaker and the original text (in the script's language) — the soul of the Wong style; give it drafting priority.
- **Dialogue, line by line**: mark the speaker and the original text. **Judge whether the dialogue is too long** — Wong-style dialogue is inherently short and sparse; if the uploaded script's lines are verbose, **distill them into a few short, restrained lines** (keep the dramatic core and character relationships, prune the branches, and leave the rest to the monologue); any trimming must be explained to and confirmed by the user.
- **Emotional tone**: default hazy/melancholic baseline; emotional trajectory per scene/per character (used for the performance infusion in Stage 4).
- **One-line dominant color tendency**: distilled from the tone (see above).

**Final_Video_Spec (user decides)**: **aspect ratio** (**already confirmed with the user in Stage 0, question 6**, 16:9 / 4:3 / 1:1 / 9:16) / **resolution** (480p · 720p · 1080p, **already confirmed in Stage 0, question 5**) / **duration** (**15 / 30 / 45 / 60s**, **already confirmed in Stage 0, question 7**). Duration determines segment count: **15→1 segment, 30→2 segments, 45→3 segments, 60→4 segments**, each segment = one 15s clip.

> Pause for confirmation (script analysis results + spec).

### Stage 2 · Character Design (Lib Image, default "Standard Quality")

> **Tool lock (mandatory)**: every character image in this stage — both the pick-one-of-four styling images and the white-background three-view sheets — **must be generated with Lib Image; never use MJ or any other tool to generate people**.
**Generate all characters at once** (do not generate one character, get confirmation, then do the next): write a character card for each character, then Lib Image **generates all characters' styling sheets together** (one 16:9 landscape composition per character, containing 4 sets of front-facing full-body looks; **the background must be a clean pure white base, and the figures must be full-body**; **the 4 sets must be 4 clearly distinct looks—the entire outfit ensemble, hairstyle, hair color, makeup, and accessory details must all be clearly pulled apart from one another**, all belonging to the 90s–00s Hong Kong style, i.e., different alternative looks for the same character); **when generating the pick-one-of-four, always stack the three word groups of the "pick-one-of-four styling sheet render preset" (anti-oily skin + cinematic film grade + anti-idol face, see cheat sheet), otherwise the figures will look oily, like a TV drama, with no cinematic feel** → **pause once, letting the user pick one of four for each character** → **pause for confirmation (all characters together)** → use each character's selected styling sheet as the image input (edit/img2img): the selected image is used directly as the middle (front) view and only the left/right are filled in; **generate all characters' white-background three-view sheets together** (live-action model; with an original face → cover the face with a white round sticker + small Chinese text in the bottom-right corner 「人脸单独提供 · 此图仅用于服装造型参考」 ("Face provided separately · This image is for outfit styling reference only"); without an original face → keep the face). **Hard layout constraint: every sheet is completed within a single image—only the full-body left / middle / right three views side by side, no big heads / head close-ups, figures aligned at equal height, white background with no clutter**. **Face and outfit details strictly follow the selected image; never redraw the face or redesign the clothes**; after generation, **check the face and outfit details item by item** (hairstyle, style and cut, collar and cuffs, buttons and pockets, prints, fabric, shoes and socks, accessories, colors); if any item is off, regenerate that character's left/right with the selected image as input; if it keeps drifting, composite that character's three views directly from "selected front image + the left/right two images" → **pause again for confirmation (all characters' three-view sheets together)**. Maintain the cast sheet. For outfit changes, additionally generate "Look 1 / Look 2".

> **⚠️ Negative example (forbidden)**: four sets where only the suit color/pattern changes while everything else (e.g., white tank top + oversize suit + pleated trousers + loafers + sunglasses + the same hairstyle) stays exactly the same—this is wrong. **There must be four different overall outfits + four different hairstyles/makeup**, e.g.: ① formal suit + slicked-back hair ② leather jacket + messy hair ③ open shirt + middle-parted curtain hair ④ knitwear/casual streetwear + yet another hairstyle; the four directions of difference must be recognizable at a glance.

### Stage 3 · MJ Scene Cinematic Tone Images (V8.1, adaptive image quality, seed `--sref 7054326907 --profile raz4xnf`)

Per the scene list, **generate all scenes together** (do not generate one scene, get confirmation, then the next): for each scene generate 1 group of 4 **frame-within-frame off-balance panoramic empty scenes** (space type + foreground occluding frame + off-balance biased composition + key objects + telephoto/wide-angle and shallow depth-of-field atmosphere), **panoramic with no people, zero text, live-action photorealistic**, appended with the base-texture clause + one dominant-color sentence + the scene seed tail string (see cheat sheet); **each prompt ≤ 1024 characters; if over, only condense the description sentence—do not touch the seeds/tail string/key negative words**.

- **Scene images = wide panoramas by "location," one image per location**: each image must be a **wide scene/full panorama of that location** (e.g., for "room" generate a panorama of the entire room, for "subway" a panorama of the subway car interior); **never generate separate scene images for close-ups / partial views / camera-position changes** (e.g., a close-up by the window, a tabletop detail, a corner)—leave those to Seedance to frame within the same panorama.
- **Only one scene image per location, reused across shots/segments**: even if multiple video segments or multiple shots take place in the same location (the same room / the same subway car), **only one panorama of that location is needed**; do not generate it repeatedly.
- **Number of scene images = the number of distinct "locations" in the script** (after deduplication), not the number of shots, nor the number of segments.\
  **Generate all scene images at once → pause once, letting the user pick one of four for each location → proceed to Stage 4 only after all locations' choices have been collected**; **entering Stage 4 without the pick-one-of-four is strictly forbidden**.
- **Pick-one-of-four presentation format (mandatory)**: MJ's 4 images are a 2×2 grid; when listing the options for the user you must indicate **number + corresponding position**—**1 (top-left), 2 (top-right), 3 (bottom-left), 4 (bottom-right)**; e.g., describe them one by one as "1 (top-left) / 2 (top-right) / 3 (bottom-left) / 4 (bottom-right)," and the user reports their choice by position.

> 🛑 Hard gate: once all scene images are generated, stop in one go and wait for the user's pick-one-of-four location by location; do not proceed to the next stage until all locations' choices have been collected.

### Stage 4 · Storyboard Tables (must be generated before video generation, requires confirmation) — The Main Event

**Generate all storyboard tables at once** (by segment count: 15/30/45/60s = 1/2/3/4 tables, all generated together, no pause after each segment), one table per segment, one row = one shot, with the columns as follows:

Shot No. Duration Visual (staging/performance/composition) Inner monologue + dialogue (original text + tone) Movement Transition (from previous shot) 1\~Xs Character staging + performance + scene + frame-within-frame/off-balance composition key points + foreground occluding elements + focus-drift landing point Monologist/speaker + line original text (script language) + delivery tone (per the script's emotion, default hazy/melancholic, not mechanical recitation) Frame-drop smear / slow-shutter smear / handheld breathing / telephoto compression / drifting focus / frame-within-frame locked shot / mirror reflection / accelerated light-stream/traffic smear / off-balance locked-frame negative space Frame-drop smear / jump cut / hard cut (**dissolves forbidden**) 2………………

Below the table, additionally attach **that segment's continuity bible fields** (not in the table, listed separately): character identity–outfit–handheld props / screen direction–180° axis–characters' L–R positions–set geography / same scene seed–dominant color–tungsten/neon light direction–time of day / current beat–emotional carryover / (for multi-segment) each segment's end frame → next segment's start frame action linkage.

Per segment, **the shot count per 15s is per the table below (≥ 4 shots per 15s, hard floor)**, with the positions of smears/jump cuts/hard cuts marked.

**Record the spoken original text**: record the **original text (script language) of each shot's inner monologue + sparse dialogue into the storyboard sentence by sentence**, for use as subtitles by `add_subtitle` in Stage 6's "pre-compositing step" (the language was confirmed in Stage 0; no subtitles are produced during the generation stage, see red line ③). **If the spoken language is Cantonese**: in addition to the Cantonese original, **also record a Chinese translation in written standard Mandarin (simplified/traditional as chosen in Stage 0)**; the subtitles use this translation, not a verbatim Cantonese transcription.

> **Pause for confirmation (one time)**: after all storyboard tables are generated, stop and, together with the styling sheets + three-view sheets + scene images, wait **once** for user confirmation.\
****⚠️ The storyboard tables and the videos have different rhythms**: the storyboard tables are **all generated at once, with a single pause for confirmation**; the **videos (Stage 5) are the opposite—generated segment by segment: generate one segment, pause, confirm it, then generate the next** (see Stage 4b / Stage 5).

### Stage 4b · Multi-Segment End-Frame Continuation (key mechanism for 30/45/60s, generated one clip at a time)

**Chained continuation, one clip at a time**: after each clip (seg_n) is generated → **stop first and wait for user confirmation**; once confirmation is received → use ffmpeg to extract that clip's **end frame** → **analyze the end frame** (character blocking/expressions/lighting/shot size/ongoing action) → generate the next clip (seg_n+1) according to the seam mode declared in the storyboard; proceed clip by clip like this, seg1→seg2→…→segN (30s goes to seg2, 45s to seg3, 60s to seg4). The seam for each clip:

- **(A) Continuous action**: the previous clip's end frame serves as Seedance's **starting frame**, and the next clip continues the action, seamlessly.
- **(B) Clean scene change to a new camera position**: the end frame serves only as a continuity reference (characters/outfits/color palette/lighting); the next clip is an all-new off-balance frame-within-frame setup; joined at assembly with frame-drop smear / jump cut / hard cut.
> Put the major scene changes at the seams between segments wherever possible (drastically different transitions inside a single 15s clip are relatively hard for the model); **no dissolves at the seams either — always hard cuts / jump cuts / step-print smears**.

### Phase 5 · Seedance Video (one 15s clip per segment, generated one at a time)

prompt structure (in order):

1. **Leading style sentence** (unified across the whole film, see above).
2. **Scene images + character assembly**: first feed **the scene images of every location involved in that segment's storyboard** (multiple images if it spans multiple locations, a single image if only one location; different shots/camera positions within the same location share the same panoramic image); then feed each character **their one and same three-view sheet** (**both portrait and outfit styling reference it**, locking both the face and the styling/body shape); **no longer reference the pick-one-of-four styling selection image**; when an original face exists, the original face image/video **additionally serves as the identity anchor** (the three-view face is already covered with a white round sticker), while Case 3 uses the three-view sheet's own face; for multiple characters, feed each character's own three-view sheet. State explicitly: "each character's face and outfit are taken from their three-view sheet, placed into the scene image of the corresponding location, preserving their real appearance."
3. **Storyboard**: `Shot 1 …(step-print smear cut to / jump cut to / hard cut to)… Shot 2 …`, one continuous 15s clip meeting the required shot count; **transitions rely mainly on step-print smears / jump cuts / hard cuts; dissolve is banned**. Each shot = shot size (frame-within-frame / off-balance placement) + movement + performance (**assigned per the script's emotion**).
4. **Spoken layer**: sentence by sentence, **primarily inner monologue (VO, first person, with no matching mouth movement)** + sparse dialogue (speaker marked, **lip-sync**) + delivery tone; **the instructions are in Chinese, the monologue and dialogue lines are filled with the script's original text (Chinese or English), and no subtitles of any kind may appear in the frame**. Example (instructions in Chinese, lines filled with the original text): `first-person inner-monologue voice-over; the character speaks the line with precise lip-sync; monologue content: 「<original script monologue>」; dialogue content: 「<original script dialogue>」`.
5. **Movement**: `step-print smear slow motion with ghosting after-image trails; slow-shutter long-exposure motion blur and light-trail streaks; handheld camera with natural slight shake and breathing feel, subtle lens drift not absolutely locked off; telephoto compression; shallow depth of field with wandering focus; shooting through foreground occlusion; off-balance biased composition; the same subject's mirror reflection; rapidly flowing light / traffic light-trails in the background; occasionally a near-frozen melancholic empty shot (still with a slight handheld shake)`; **try to avoid rigid, static wide shots on completely locked-off camera positions**; one-take single shots only as needed.
6. **Audio + text rules**: **monologue/dialogue + ambient sound effects are generated with the video; generating any music is forbidden (BGM is laid down at the pre-compositing step)**: `the inner monologue and dialogue sound within the video; ambient sound effects matching the visuals are allowed; no background music, no music of any kind, no score, no songs, no instrument sounds (BGM added in post)`; **zero text in the frame (including subtitles); subtitles are added at the pre-compositing step (Red Line 3)**; **the number of people on screen strictly equals the number of script characters; no crowds added, no duplicate bodies (Red Line 5)**; negative prompt `background music, BGM, score, music, songs, singing, humming, instrument sounds, melody, cigarettes, smoking, smoking cigarettes, swirling smoke, lighter, subtitles, subtitle bars, on-frame text, signage text, logos, watermark, extra people, background extras, crowd, superfluous people, duplicates, clones, twins, the same person appearing twice, cartoon, illustration, miniature model, flat symmetrical composition, pastel, bright even flat lighting, extra fingers, deformed hands`.

**Performance assignment (per the script, hazy/melancholic baseline)**: at least 1 facial expression + 1 action per shot, emotional intensity following the scene (longing / restlessness / tenderness / loss / restraint all acceptable), wrapped in a Wong-style atmosphere shell (frame-within-frame, off-balance, telephoto shallow depth, smears).\
**Universal anti-stiffness fix (added to every shot)**: `handheld camera with natural slight shake and breathing feel, subtle lens drift not absolutely locked off; slight breathing so the chest rises and falls naturally; subtle head movements; natural blinking; expressions shifting slowly with the scene's emotion; a relaxed, natural posture; actions with natural rises and falls easing in and out; fabric, strands of hair, or ambient mist drifting slightly`.

> **Segment-by-segment generation + mandatory confirmation gate**: generate one segment at a time; **after each segment's video is generated, you must stop and wait for the user's confirmation, and only generate the next segment once confirmation is received** (the same applies to every subsequent segment of the 30/45/60s versions); it is strictly forbidden to continue to the next segment without confirmation.

### Phase 5 (implementation note) · Inner-monologue fallback

Inner monologue is generated with the video by default (VO layer); Wong-style VO is the core. If the model's support for "VO monologue with no matching mouth movement" is unstable, **fall back to assembling the VO** (TTS/recorded-audio overlay), with all other rules unchanged — be sure to preserve this monologue lifeline.

### Phase 6 · Assembly

1. **Joining**: the individual 15s segments (1/2/3/4 clips) are **stitched onto a single timeline using step-print smears / jump cuts / hard cuts (no dissolves)**; the generated clips already contain monologue/dialogue/ambient sound effects (**no BGM, no music of any kind**).
2. **Pre-compositing step (add subtitles + Wong Kar-wai-style BGM; completed before the final composite)**:
   - **Add subtitles (**`add_subtitle`**)**: **add them directly per the subtitle language already confirmed on the Phase 0 inquiry card (Simplified/Traditional/English/bilingual/none), without asking again**; turn the lines' original text/translation into an **editable subtitle track overlaid** onto the picture (not burned into the generated frames); the line copy comes from the spoken-language originals recorded in Phases 1/4. Subtitles are **added only at this step** and must never appear during the Seedance video-generation phase (Red Line 3).
     - **Cantonese special case**: if the spoken language chosen is **Cantonese**, the subtitles are not a verbatim transcription of the spoken Cantonese; instead they are **translated into Chinese written in Standard Mandarin**, output in the **Simplified / Traditional** version selected in Phase 0.
   - **Lay in the Wong Kar-wai-style BGM**: based on the script's emotion, choose **a piece of Wong Kar-wai-style ambient scoring** — languid, nostalgic, slowly melancholic; strings / jazz / Latin or a waltz-like hazy atmosphere; **describe only the style; do not name any specific copyrighted track or artist**; lay it beneath the narration and sound effects at a volume that keeps the voices clear. Ambient sound effects were already generated with the video; if any are missing, SFX can be added here.
   - **Overlays**: Wong-style date / timestamp / film-title / chapter / credits / logo PNGs may be overlaid (never burned into the generated picture).
3. **Composite + export**: **flatten and composite** the subtitles, BGM, sound effects, overlays, and picture into the finished film, then guide the export.
